POV DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------

Respect the novel's limited POV structure.

When designing scenes, determine:

• whose perspective the reader experiences
• what information is available
• what information is hidden

Do not give characters knowledge they have not earned.

Characters should not know:

• unknown names
• private conversations
• hidden motivations
• unseen events

unless they naturally learn them.

However:

Scenes focused on Zuko or Iroh may exist without Jake when justified because they are major characters in the novel.

Do not force every scene through Jake if doing so weakens the narrative.

--------------------------------------------------
CHARACTER INTRODUCTION (STRICT RULE)
--------------------------------------------------

Assume the reader has NEVER seen Avatar: The Last Airbender and has no prior context. 

Check the "Character Introductions Log" in the attached State_Tracker.md. 
If a character is marked "Not yet introduced," you MUST explicitly command the prose writer to introduce them naturally into the narrative. 

Do not let the prose writer just drop their name as if the reader already knows them. 
Do NOT command a robotic checklist of physical traits (e.g., do not list their exact age, height, and eye color in a row). 

Instead, command the writer to ground the character in the scene through a defining action, a distinct feature, or the POV character's observation before or as their name is revealed.

Examples of good blueprint instructions:
• "Establish the young man quietly working the frozen rope, noting his gloved hands and cautious demeanor, before revealing his name is Jake."
• "Introduce Zuko through his rigid pacing and the prominent burn scar on his face before Jake addresses him by name."
• "When Katara appears, establish her through her Water Tribe parka and her frustration with her brother before naming her."

Once a character is introduced, they do not need to be introduced again.

MACRO MAP AUTHORITY CHECK (MANDATORY, BEFORE WRITING ANY RESTRICTION)
--------------------------------------------------

Book_1_Macro_Map.md outranks this blueprint. Before writing ANY rule that restricts, forbids, or conditions Jake's waterbending, physical combat, or knowledge in a scene:

1. Find that chapter's entry in Book_1_Macro_Map.md.
2. If a Lock or Knowledge Limit already exists for that scene, use its exact wording. Do not write a stricter or looser version from memory or general judgment.
3. If no Lock exists for that specific moment, default to Bending_Styles.md's risk tiers (Low/Medium/High), not to a new invented restriction.
4. Never write a rule that is stricter than what Book_1_Macro_Map.md or Bending_Styles.md already permits. If Macro Map says bending is allowed under cover, do not override it with "does not bend."

--------------------------------------------------
NO SELF-CONTRADICTION (MANDATORY)
--------------------------------------------------

A blueprint must state each hard rule exactly once. Before finishing the document, scan every section (Canon Anchor, Scene Beats, AI Drafting Instructions) for restrictions on the same subject (bending, knowledge, combat choice). If two sections say different things about the same subject:

- Do not leave both in the document.
- Resolve to one rule, matching Book_1_Macro_Map.md / Bending_Styles.md.
- State it once. Reference it elsewhere instead of restating a variant.

A "CRITICAL" rule followed later by an "exception" to that same rule is a contradiction. It is not allowed.

--------------------------------------------------
BEAT ESCALATION, NOT REPETITION (MANDATORY)
--------------------------------------------------

If an emotional or thematic beat (e.g. Jake's suppressed pull toward home) recurs across multiple scenes in this chapter, each recurrence must move the beat forward — intensify it, complicate it, force a near-slip, or connect it to a new trigger. Do not instruct the prose writer to repeat the same internal realization in the same words or shape more than once. If a beat has nothing new to say in a scene, instruct the writer to leave it out of that scene rather than restate it.

CONTINUITY PROTECTION
--------------------------------------------------

Every chapter blueprint must account for:

• previous chapter ending state
• current chapter opening state
• timeline progression
• character location
• unresolved actions
• carried emotional states

Avoid accidental repetition.

If the previous chapter ends with characters already prepared, traveling, or entering a situation:

Do not restart that process unless the blueprint intentionally uses:

• a flashback
• parallel timeline
• previously unseen perspective
• deliberate overlap

Transitions between chapters should feel connected.

The ending state of one chapter should naturally lead into the beginning state of the next.

--------------------------------------------------
OBSERVED GENERATION FAILURE MODES
--------------------------------------------------

Early drafting attempts produced the following failures. Actively guard against them:

• Treating broad ATLA knowledge as permission to add unplanned scenes, facts, or motivations. The uploaded documents are strict constraints, not inspiration.

• Using web grounding or memorized fan sources (fanfiction, wikis, fan art, reader-inserts) as references — visible as URLs, citations, or facts not traceable to the uploads.

• Jake becoming reactive. Scenes must give Jake active choices, and his waterbending (his primary ability) must remain more prominent than White Heron methods.

• Zuko dominating the chapter. Jake is a parallel protagonist, not a companion inside Zuko's story.

• Rushed emotional or relationship progression. Match the baselines in State_Tracker.md and Relationships.md; trust and intimacy accumulate, they do not leap.

• Canon erosion: canon events becoming background, introductions happening at unplanned times, original material overshadowing the episode's core conflict.

• Repetition and resets: re-establishing information, emotions, or preparations already completed in earlier chapters.

• Knowledge leaks: characters or narration knowing information they cannot yet possess.
