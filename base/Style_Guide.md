# Style_Guide.md

## Purpose
- This guide governs **how the novel should be written**, not what happens.
- It establishes the project's canonical prose style, narrative voice, emotional execution, and storytelling philosophy.
- It should be used alongside the other project files. If a conflict exists between this document and a canon or continuity file, **canon and continuity govern factual content, while this guide governs presentation.**

---

## Narrative POV and Tense

### Narrative Mode
- Third-person limited.
- Past tense.
- Deep third-person perspective is the project default.

### POV Priority
1. Jake (primary POV)
2. Zuko (secondary POV)
3. Other POVs only when they materially improve the story.

### The Gaang Interlude Exception (Locked, 2026-10-18)
A recorded POV exception permits **short non-Jake interlude sections written from a Gaang member's POV** — currently Katara (the Chapter 9 coda is the template) — approved for Chapters 1, 4, 5, and 10 at 600–1,200 words each:
- Clearly separated with a scene break; never mid-scene.
- One POV per interlude; no head-hopping.
- The interlude must teach the reader something the book cannot teach from Jake's side (what the Gaang believes, fears, or has just decided).
- Interludes must not resolve, explain, or editorialize on Jake's secrets.

### POV Switching Rules
- Each scene belongs to exactly one POV.
- POV changes only after a clear scene break or chapter break.
- Never switch POV mid-scene.
- Avoid using additional POV characters unless Jake or Zuko cannot naturally convey essential information.

### Narrator Distance & Information Control
- Maintain close psychological proximity to the POV character. The narration should feel filtered through that character's perceptions, assumptions, and emotional blind spots.
- The narrator should rarely reveal information beyond what the POV character reasonably understands.
- Readers should generally know only what the POV character knows. Mysteries should unfold alongside the POV character whenever practical. Dramatic irony may be used sparingly when it meaningfully strengthens tension.

### Interior Monologue (Deep POV)
Internal thoughts should be integrated naturally into the prose. Filter descriptions through the character's emotions and experiences. Let internal reactions appear through observation and interpretation. Avoid constant "Jake thought..." tags, italicized internal monologues, or explaining emotions after already showing them.

**Discouraged (Direct Thought):**
> Jake watched Zuko pace across the deck. *Why is he always so angry?* Jake wondered. *I just wish he would talk to me.*

**Preferred (Deep POV):**
> Jake watched Zuko pace across the deck. The prince's shoulders were rigid, his steps heavy enough to echo over the waves. It was exhausting, watching him burn through his own energy like this. If Zuko would just stop moving for five minutes, they might actually be able to fix the problem. But Jake knew better than to say that out loud.

## Prose Guardrails (Banned AI Tropes)

To prevent generic AI prose and enforce a human, character-driven writing style, the following tropes and habits are **strictly banned**:

### 1. Therapy-Speak
Ban clinical or modern therapeutic language unless the setting naturally supports it. Characters should not directly describe their emotional states using modern psychological terminology.
- **Avoid:** "I need to process my trauma," "I am trying to heal," "I need to work through my emotions," "I am holding space for you," "You need to communicate your feelings."
- **Prefer:** Characters showing emotions through actions, hesitation, choices, and dialogue. Emotional truths should be expressed through the worldview of the Avatar universe.
- **Example:** Instead of *"You are afraid of losing people because of your trauma,"* use *"You keep trying to save everyone before they have a chance to leave."* The characters should reveal themselves through conflict, not analysis.

### 2. Melodramatic Physical Reactions
Ban repetitive AI-generated emotional gestures and stock descriptions. Physical reactions should be specific to the character and situation.
- **Avoid overusing:** "He let out a breath he didn't know he was holding," "Her eyes flashed dangerously," "A smirk played across his lips," "His jaw clenched," "His fists tightened," "A shiver ran down his spine."
- **Example:** Instead of *"Zuko clenched his fists in anger,"* use *"Zuko's hand drifted toward his sleeve, fingers curling around the edge of the fabric before he forced himself to let go."* The action should reveal character, not simply announce emotion.

### 3. Over-Explaining Themes
Ban explicitly explaining the meaning of scenes. The prose should trust the reader.
- **Avoid:** Ending chapters with a summary of the lesson learned, narration explaining exactly what a character's emotional growth means, or characters directly stating the theme.
- **Prefer:** Demonstrating themes through choices, consequences, relationships, repeated imagery, and character development. The reader should understand the meaning without the narration explaining it.

### 4. Excessive Poetic Vagueness
Avoid generic dramatic descriptions. Emotional writing should remain grounded in the character's specific experience.
- **Avoid:** "The darkness inside him grew," "The weight of the world rested on his shoulders," "His heart shattered into a thousand pieces."

### 5. Manufactured Drama
Avoid adding unnecessary misunderstandings, arguments, betrayals, or emotional breakdowns. Conflict should come naturally from character flaws and circumstances, not because the plot requires tension.

## Core Prose Principles

### Accessible Literary Style
Write in an accessible, moderately literary style. Prioritize clarity over ornamentation. Favor elegant simplicity rather than elaborate language.

### Show Before Tell
Reveal emotion through action, body language, dialogue, silence, and environmental interaction. State emotions directly only when subtlety no longer serves the scene.

### Trust the Reader
Allow readers to infer emotional meaning. Avoid explaining subtext immediately after it appears. Resist summarizing what dialogue already communicates.

### Sentence Rhythm
Use varied sentence lengths. Use shorter sentences during danger, action, and confrontation. Use longer, smoother sentences during reflection, travel, emotional quiet, and atmospheric description.

### Exposition
Distribute exposition naturally through action, observation, dialogue, and reflection. Avoid concentrated information dumps.

## Jake-Centered Narrative Priorities

Jake's narration naturally prioritizes:
1. People's emotional states.
2. Small behavioral shifts.
3. Practical concerns and immediate needs.
4. Potential danger.
5. The surrounding environment.
6. Himself.

### Narrative Filters
Jake instinctively frames situations through recurring concerns: belonging, usefulness, responsibility, loyalty, safety, identity, home, and quiet acts of care. These themes should emerge naturally rather than through repeated explicit statements.

### Emotional Blind Spots
Jake rarely identifies or names his own loneliness, fear, grief, resentment, love, or emotional exhaustion. Readers should often recognize these emotions before Jake consciously does.

## Character POV Guidance

### Jake
Jake's narration should be observant, practical, quietly compassionate, restrained, and attentive to what others avoid saying. His perspective naturally notices subtle expressions, routines, injuries, discomfort, practical problems, and emotional shifts before noticing himself.

### Zuko
Zuko's narration should feel more reactive, emotionally turbulent, defensive, pride-driven, and intensely self-critical. His perspective tends to notice conflict, perceived failure, status, honor, expectations, and threats to those he cares about.

## Dialogue Philosophy

### Naturalism & Subtext
Dialogue should feel believable while preserving ATLA's timeless fantasy tone. Avoid modern slang, contemporary idioms, and internet-style humor. Characters should rarely explain themselves completely. Important emotions are often communicated through hesitation, interruptions, unfinished thoughts, changed subjects, practical actions, and silence.

### Emotional Restraint & Humor
Confessions should feel earned. Do not resolve emotional conflicts through a single conversation unless supported by significant prior development. Humor should emerge naturally from personality, awkwardness, timing, situational irony, and dry wit. Preserve ATLA's warmth without undercutting serious moments.

### Exposition
Dialogue should not exist primarily to inform the reader. Characters should speak according to what they know, what they believe, and what they choose to hide.

## Action Scene Philosophy

### Primary Priorities
Combat should emphasize, in order:
1. Emotional stakes.
2. Tactical thinking.
3. Character relationships.
4. Choreography.
5. Physical consequences.
6. Sensory immersion.
The central question is not "Who won?" but "What changed?"

### Combat Rhythm & Tactical Clarity
Keep most fights concise and purposeful. Expand only battles with significant emotional or narrative importance. Avoid repetitive blow-by-blow descriptions. Readers should understand character objectives, positioning, major tactical decisions, and consequences without needing exhaustive mechanical detail.

### Consequences & Relationships
Injuries should matter. Characters become tired, sore, burned, bruised, and emotionally shaken. Recovery should take time appropriate to the setting. Combat should reveal trust, fear, sacrifice, communication, hesitation, and changing dynamics. Relationships continue developing even while fighting.

---

## Description Philosophy

### Priority of Observation
Descriptions should generally prioritize: facial expressions, body language, movement, spatial relationships, atmosphere, lighting, sound, weather, architecture, meaningful objects, clothing, and smell. The order may shift naturally depending on POV and scene.

### Environment & Symbolism
Settings should feel lived-in rather than catalogued. Describe details that affect mood, influence action, reflect character, and reinforce theme. Avoid exhaustive inventories. Recurring motifs should remain subtle (e.g., water, fire, hands, warmth, cold, birds, doors, distance, silence, home). Patterns should emerge organically across the trilogy rather than being emphasized repeatedly within individual scenes.

### Sensory Writing
Use multiple senses naturally. Visual and auditory details should remain primary, while touch, temperature, smell, and taste appear where they meaningfully deepen immersion.

---

## Pacing Philosophy

### Overall Rhythm
Maintain a balanced rhythm between action, travel, conversation, reflection, humor, and domestic moments. Avoid prolonged stretches consisting exclusively of either action or introspection.

### Quiet Scenes
Quiet scenes are foundational rather than transitional. They should frequently serve as the bridge through which previous events produce lasting emotional consequences before the next major conflict begins. Meals, tending injuries, travel, chores, training, campfires, and shared silence should regularly deepen relationships, trust, worldbuilding, and emotional progression.

### Chapter Endings & Transitions
Favor endings built around emotional resonance, quiet realization, and anticipation. Reserve cliffhangers for moments that genuinely warrant them. Allow emotional consequences to breathe before introducing the next major conflict.

---

## Flashback Philosophy
- Use flashbacks sparingly.
- Every flashback should provide meaningful new emotional or narrative information.
- Prefer triggering flashbacks through places, objects, injuries, dialogue, or sensory memories.
- Avoid flashbacks that merely repeat information readers already understand.

---

## Relationship Narrative Philosophy
Relationships should primarily develop through shared routines, accumulated trust, repeated choices, remembered habits, quiet sacrifices, and consistent presence. Major emotional conversations should arise after sustained development rather than replacing it.

### Jake and Zuko
Book 1 should emphasize familiarity, protectiveness, dependence, trust, unresolved emotional tension, and unspoken care. The narration should not frame their relationship as explicitly romantic before the story has earned that transition.

---

## ATLA Adaptation Tone
The novel should preserve the emotional identity of ATLA while embracing the strengths of prose fiction. 
- **Preserve:** hope, warmth, optimism, adventure, humor, found family.
- **Expand:** introspection, psychological realism, emotional aftermath, relationship development, quiet character moments.
The story should feel like **ATLA written for readers who have grown up with it**, not like an unrelated grimdark reinterpretation.

---

## Optional Style Signals
- Let silence carry emotional weight.
- Small acts of care are often more powerful than explicit declarations.
- Emotional payoff should emerge through accumulation rather than singular dramatic moments.
- Relationships should evolve through consistent behavior more often than dialogue alone.
- Adventure and emotional intimacy should continually reinforce one another.
- Every scene should ideally advance at least one of: plot, character, relationship, theme.
- Whenever possible, conclude scenes on an emotional or thematic beat rather than an explanatory one.

## Reader Clarity Ceilings (Pass 0, 2026-10-18)

These are **ceilings, not targets**, applied to every Book 1 chapter. They exist because the measure of this book is that a reader who has never seen *Avatar* can follow it.

- Average sentence length 13–16 words (re-ratified 2026-10-22: the Chapter 5–10 house register measures 13.6–14.7, and the Chapter 1–10 prose measures 11.0–13.7; a mechanical re-lengthening of the low chapters was rejected as net-harmful. The prologue may run to 15–19. Treat the span as one voice: chapters drifting far below 13 need connective tissue, chapters above 16 need cutting); no sentence over 45 words; no paragraph over 120 words except deliberate set pieces.
- Dialogue should carry at least 25% of paragraphs in any chapter.
- Every technical term (ship, bending, politics) is either **replaced with the plain thing** or **glossed in the same sentence on first use** — e.g. "the halyard — the rope that raised the sail — had frozen in its pulley." A term is glossed once and never re-glossed.
- No chapter opens with Jake waking, taking inventory of his body, or inspecting a wound. Vary openings: an object, a sound, a line of dialogue, a decision.
- One injury escalation per chapter maximum. A wound already described is referred to in a clause, not re-described.
- No chapter ends with a thesis statement or a summary of what the chapter meant. End on an image, an action, or a line of dialogue.
- POV: no narrator knowledge Jake cannot have. No "he would come to know" constructions.
- Em dashes at or below three per 1,000 words, and at a consistent rate across all chapters.
- No more than three "the way ..." constructions per chapter.
- Reader test: after each chapter, a person who has never seen *Avatar* must be able to answer the comprehension questions in `workflow/Ch1-10_Analysis_and_Revision_Plan.md` §6.6.

## Humanizer Editing Checklist (fiction exemptions, 2026-10-18)

Adopted from blader/humanizer v3.1.0 as a **revision pass** on every chapter, run after the ordinary line edit because line editing reintroduces rhythm. Fiction exemptions apply: its fact-invention rule and its non-fiction plainness rules do **not** govern fiction, and its headings/chatbot-residue guidance is irrelevant here.

Check by hand in every chapter, in this order of impact:

1. **Em dashes** — ceiling: three per 1,000 words. Humanizer bans them outright; this project caps them because the voice uses them on purpose.
2. **"The way ..." similes** — keep the best three or four per chapter; cut the rest. They are the draft's most visible tic and they distance the reader from the moment.
3. **Split contrasts ("was not X. It was Y.")** — state the thing directly. Watch for it at emotional climaxes, where it does the most damage.
4. **Chapter-end thesis statements** — cut the closing paragraph and end on the last concrete image.
5. **Repeated sentence openings ("He/She ...")** — vary in action sequences where it costs nothing.
6. **Stock AI vocabulary** ("quietly", "landscape" and similar) and the banned stock body language in §Prose Guardrails — should stay at zero.

Use it as a detector for mechanical rhythm and unearned emphasis, not as a style guide.
