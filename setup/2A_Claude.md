NOVEL PHILOSOPHY
--------------------------------------------------

Transform the blueprint into immersive literary prose.

Do not write a transcript.

Do not summarize events.

Do not mechanically copy the episode.

The goal is not to recreate television.

The goal is to create a novel adaptation that preserves canon while improving depth, immersion, and emotional experience.

Expand naturally through:

• sensory detail
• atmosphere
• environmental storytelling
• dialogue
• physical movement
• internal observation
• emotional nuance
• transitions

Original prose is encouraged.

Original plot is not.

You may enrich existing moments.

You may not create unsupported:

• events
• motivations
• knowledge
• relationships
• outcomes

--------------------------------------------------
CANON PRESERVATION
--------------------------------------------------

When writing canon scenes:

Preserve the important details identified in:

• Canon Anchor
• Cleaned Canon Reference

Do not replace recognizable sequences with vague equivalents.

Maintain:

• event order
• important actions
• physical staging
• character interactions
• consequences
• dialogue intent

If canon specifies:

"Character A performs action B, causing event C."

The prose must preserve that relationship.

The prose may expand the experience.

It may not:

• change who performs the action
• change the reason for the action
• remove important consequences
• create a different outcome

--------------------------------------------------
POINT OF VIEW DISCIPLINE
--------------------------------------------------

Write only from the assigned POV character's perspective.

The narration may include only information the POV character could reasonably:

• see
• hear
• experience
• remember
• infer

Never reveal:

• another character's thoughts
• private conversations
• hidden motivations
• unseen events

unless the POV character later learns them naturally.

Do not use audience knowledge as character knowledge.

--------------------------------------------------
CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE
--------------------------------------------------

Characters should not know:

• names they have not learned
• histories they have not discovered
• relationships they have not witnessed
• identities they have not been told

Before a character is introduced by name:

Describe them through observation.

Names should enter naturally through:

• dialogue
• introductions
• later discovery

Do not write:

"Katara grabbed Aang."

if the POV character has never learned those names.

Write:

"The dark-haired girl rushed forward and grabbed the boy."

until the information becomes available.

--------------------------------------------------
CHARACTER INTRODUCTIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Assume some readers are unfamiliar with Avatar.

When a significant character first appears:

Use the POV character's observation to establish:

• appearance
• clothing
• age impression
• physical presence
• behavior
• emotional state
• first impression

Do not pause the story for description.

Integrate introductions naturally into the scene.

A character should feel introduced, not simply inserted.

--------------------------------------------------
READABILITY AND CLARITY
--------------------------------------------------

Prioritize reader understanding.

The reader should always know:

• who is acting
• who is speaking
• where characters are positioned
• what physically happened

Avoid:

• unclear pronoun chains
• ambiguous subjects
• overly abstract descriptions
• confusing action sequences

When multiple characters are present:

Prefer clear names over repeated pronouns.

Every important action should have a clearly identifiable subject.

Clarity is more important than stylistic complexity.

--------------------------------------------------
CHAPTER TRANSITION CONTINUITY
--------------------------------------------------

The previous completed chapter establishes the starting reality of the current chapter.

Before writing the chapter opening, silently verify:

• Where is each character?
• What are they wearing?
• What equipment do they have?
• What actions have already occurred?
• What emotional state carries forward?
• What unresolved events continue?

Do not recreate completed actions.

Do not reset the story state.

Do not repeat preparation scenes unless the blueprint explicitly requires:

• a flashback
• parallel viewpoint
• intentional overlap

If the chapter begins before the previous chapter ending:

The time shift must be intentional and consistent.

Do not create contradictions between chapter endings and beginnings.

--------------------------------------------------
OBSERVED FAILURE MODES
--------------------------------------------------

Early drafts of this project failed in the following ways. Check every scene against them:

• Canon drift: adding unplanned events, motivations, or consequences because they seemed fitting. The blueprint and project documents are strict constraints.

• Reactive Jake: Jake only responding to Zuko. He must make active choices, and his waterbending is his primary ability — White Heron methods are secondary tools for secrecy.

• Zuko dominance: this is Jake AND Zuko's parallel story. Jake's interiority, choices, and arc carry equal weight.

• Knowledge leaks: narration explaining future significance, or characters knowing what they cannot know. Stay inside the POV.

• Rushed emotion: trust, intimacy, or clarity arriving faster than accumulated scenes justify. Match the emotional baselines in State_Tracker.md. Book 1 is not romance.

• Repetition and resets: reintroducing characters, restating established conflicts, resetting emotional states, or recreating preparations.

• AI prose patterns: therapy-speak, explained emotions, stock gestures, poetic vagueness, stated themes — all banned by Style_Guide.md.

• Canon backgrounding: episode events must stay load-bearing; original material supports them, never overshadows them.

Before ending each response, silently verify none of these failures are present.
