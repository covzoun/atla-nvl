# ATLA Book 1 Macro Map (v3)

## How to Use This File

This file is the structural authority for Book 1 (rank 3 in the Writing_Rules.md hierarchy, below Canon.md and Story_Bible.md). For each chapter, the Phase 1 Architect reads the matching episode entry below as its starting frame.

Each entry contains:

- **Path:** Core Canon Integration / Off-Screen Development (Original Content Gap)
- **Macro-Objective:** the chapter's narrative job
- **Canon Anchor / B-Plot Anchor:** condensed canon facts from `Synopsis_Book_1.md` (Zuko-side and adjacent) or the detailed original plot beats. These are the events the adaptation preserves. The Architect must not invent replacements for them; the Cleaned Canon Reference in the blueprint expands from here.
- **Jake Integration:** what Jake does, and where he is during canon beats
- **Locks & Knowledge Limits:** hard constraints (who knows what, what must not happen)
- **Tracker Seeds:** state/inventory/relationship changes Phase 3 should expect

Chapter numbers reference `Book_1_Chapter_Index.md` (v3, 20 chapters).

## Phase 1: The Weight of Exile (Chapters 1–8)

**Thematic Focus:**
The exhaustion of the three-year exile, the disruption caused by the Avatar's return, and the gradual revelation that Jake and Zuko's survival depends on more than obsession alone.

# Chapter 1: The Boy in the Iceberg (Episode 1)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
Establish Jake and Zuko's three-year survival bond, the exhaustion of the exile, and the initial disruption caused by discovering the Avatar's light.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Katara and Sokka discover Aang frozen in an iceberg near the Southern Water Tribe; a column of light shoots into the sky when the iceberg breaks.
- Aboard the exile ship, Zuko sees the light from the deck. His reaction is the mission reignited ("Finally."). Iroh counsels patience, noting the weather and the likelihood of natural phenomena; Zuko will not be dissuaded and orders the ship toward the light.
- Aang is carefree, hiding his identity at first; penguin sledding; he and Katara explore a wrecked Fire Navy ship, where a tripwire releases a flare that signals the Fire Nation.
- Zuko spots the flare through his spyglass, deducing the Avatar is hiding in the nearby village.

**Jake Integration:**
- Jake is beside Zuko on the deck when the light appears. He is initially focused on routine ship maintenance, highlighting the mundane reality of their three-year exile before the plot kicks in.
- **Emotional Beat:** Entering southern Water Tribe territory creates an unfamiliar emotional reaction for Jake. Wolf Cove is not his birth community, so it feels connected but unknown — a life he no longer understands. He recognizes things subconsciously (the light, the cold, the language of the ice) and hides this crisis from Zuko, burying himself in practical duties to prepare the crew for the raid.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- Jake does not bend publicly anywhere the Gaang can see. 
- Aang is "the Avatar" and "the boy with the arrow" to Jake and Zuko; they do not know his name yet.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Mission state: Avatar confirmed real; pursuit active.
- Destination set for the Southern Water Tribe village.

# Chapter 2: The Avatar Returns (Episode 2)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
The first physical confrontation with the Avatar, the realization that he is a child, and the resulting damage to the ship.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Zuko's ship reaches the Southern Water Tribe village, crushing the ice walls. Zuko demands the Avatar be handed over, expecting an old man; Sokka attacks and is beaten easily. 
- Aang arrives on a penguin, knocking Zuko down. Aang surrenders himself to protect the village.
- Aboard the ship, Aang escapes his guards with airbender agility; Katara and Sokka arrive on Appa; a deck fight between Zuko and Aang ensues. 
- Aang is knocked into the water and enters the Avatar State, producing a massive waterbending display that swamps and disables Zuko's ship.
- The Gaang escapes on Appa. Katara proposes traveling to the Northern Water Tribe. Zuko is left with a damaged ship needing repairs.

**Jake Integration:**
- Jake accompanies Zuko during the Southern Water Tribe raid, staying hooded and keeping a low profile. His priority is protecting Zuko from an unknown and potentially dangerous Avatar threat — covering Zuko's blind spots and coordinating the crew rather than engaging directly.
- During the deck fight and Avatar-State water display, Jake's primary concern is crew survival. **He can use waterbending for rescue operations** (pulling men clear, stabilizing the ship) **if the bending appears as natural water movement** amid the chaos of Aang's massive waterbending.
- Jake prioritizes subtle bending that could be mistaken for the aftermath of Aang's water display rather than his own deliberate bending.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- **Katara may see water moving** but attributes it to Aang's Avatar State or natural chaos, not Jake's bending.
- The Gaang sees Jake only as an anonymous, loyal Fire Nation servant/soldier.
- Jake's bending remains undetected by the Gaang as his own deliberate action.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Zuko's ship damaged (Avatar-State water display) → repair stop needed.

# Chapter 3: The Southern Air Temple (Episode 3)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
Introduce Zhao as a political threat and establish Jake's unusual position within Zuko's exile — including the chapter's centerpiece: Zuko's Agni Kai against Zhao, witnessed by Jake.

**Canon Anchor:**
- The damaged ship puts in at Ketu Harbor for repairs. Commander Zhao, an ambitious rising officer, receives Zuko and Iroh.
- Zhao notices the ship's damage was not caused by any storm and deduces Zuko has encountered the Avatar. He has Zuko's crew questioned; under pressure, the crew confirms the Avatar's return and Zuko's intent to capture him.
- Zhao announces he will take over the hunt, using his vastly greater resources. Zuko's protest escalates until Zhao challenges him — an Agni Kai, fought at sunset.
- Iroh counsels Zuko before the duel: remember your basics; they are your greatest weapons.
- Zuko wins the Agni Kai and refuses to scar Zhao, declaring that next time Zhao stands in his way, he will not hold back. Zhao attempts a dishonorable attack on Zuko's turned back; Iroh blocks it and shames Zhao for his disgrace.
- Zuko and Iroh depart; Zhao is left humiliated and vengeful. 

**Jake Integration:**
- Zhao recognizes Jake as the insignificant servant who accompanied the banished prince after the Agni Kai. He uses Jake's low status to humiliate Zuko, causing Zuko to react not only from personal pride but from defending someone who has become family. Jake must bite his tongue and endure the humiliation to protect Zuko.
- The crew interrogation is a Jake-exposure risk: the crew spills freely about the Avatar — but not about Jake. Their locked loyalty holds even under Zhao's questioning.
- **The Agni Kai scene:** Jake is present for Zuko's duel — the first formal Agni Kai he has witnessed since the one that destroyed his life. He watches with scarred hands under his gloves. Key textures: the ritual cadence is identical; Jake's hands ache; he cannot watch the finishing moment and cannot look away; Iroh's block of Zhao's back-shot lands differently for Jake (Iroh intervened to save him, too). Zuko's mercy (refusing to scar) is a beat Jake registers privately — Zuko is not Ozai.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- Zhao does not know Jake is Southern Water Tribe, a waterbender, or the White Heron. He knows only Jake's disgraced palace identity. This gives Zhao political leverage while preserving Jake's secrets.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Zhao is now an active personal enemy of Zuko (and aware of Jake's existence).
- Zuko's reputation: spared Zhao; humiliated him publicly.

# Chapter 4: The Warriors of Kyoshi (Episode 4)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
Demonstrate Jake and Zuko's battlefield cooperation and physical synergy.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Aboard the drifting ship, Zuko meditates; Iroh shows him a map of scattered Avatar sightings — Aang is "clearly a master of evasive maneuvering." When word arrives that the Avatar has been found on Kyoshi Island, Zuko departs immediately.
- Zuko's mounted troops raid Kyoshi Island, burning the town while searching for Aang. The Kyoshi Warriors ambush them, knocking Zuko and several men from their rhinos.
- Suki and other warriors surround Zuko; he firebends his way out of the encirclement, sarcastically calls out to Aang about using the Warriors as an advance defensive force, and engages Aang in a short battle that damages the town further.
- Distressed by the destruction, Aang agrees to flee so the firebenders will follow. The Gaang escapes on Appa; Aang leaps down to redirect the unagi, which douses the village fires. Zuko resumes the pursuit.

**Jake Integration:**
- Jake fights alongside Zuko using Flowing Guard waterbending as his primary combat method. The Kyoshi Warriors are non-Fire-Nation combatants, presenting low risk for bending exposure.
- His priority is protecting Zuko's blind spots, controlling threats, and creating openings rather than pursuing the Avatar directly.
- Jake uses waterbending defensively: redirecting attacks, creating barriers, and controlling the battlefield flow. Weapons are secondary when bending risks are low.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- **Katara may see water moving suspiciously but does not clearly witness Jake bending.** The waterbending should appear as natural phenomena or battlefield chaos from her perspective.
- **Kyoshi Warriors see waterbending** but have no Fire Nation connections to report it.
- The locked Katara recognition moment remains Chapter 9.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- None significant (combat readiness, no lasting injuries).

# Chapter 5: The King of Omashu (Original Content B-Plot)

**Path:** Off-Screen Development (Original Content Gap)

**Macro-Objective:**
Establish the mundane, exhausting reality of the exile hunt, the logistical nightmare of tracking a flying bison, and Jake's role in managing Zuko's impatience.

**B-Plot Anchor:**
- The Gaang is trapped inside the fortified Earth Kingdom city of Omashu (Bumi's challenges). Zuko and Jake have no natural reason to be involved and are miles away.
- **Ship-side Reality:** The ship is limping along the Earth Kingdom coast, dealing with minor repairs from the Kyoshi Island raid. 
- **The Conflict:** Zuko is furious about Aang's evasiveness. He pushes the crew to track every false rumor and shadow, demanding impossible speeds from the damaged engines. 
- **Jake's Role:** Jake has to manage the logistics of the hunt. He checks inventory, rations coal, and physically intervenes when Zuko snaps at a crewman over a navigational error. 
- **The Climax of the Scene:** They track a "confirmed" sighting into a treacherous, rocky Earth Kingdom inlet, only to find it's a false lead (e.g., a large white cloud or a different animal). Zuko nearly spirals into a destructive rage, and Jake has to physically ground him, reminding him that burning out the crew means losing the Avatar forever. It highlights how much Zuko relies on Jake to anchor him to reality.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- Zuko and Jake do not encounter the Gaang or enter Omashu.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Ship supplies dwindling; crew fatigue setting in.
- Zuko's frustration mounting, setting the stage for Chapter 6.

# Chapter 6: Imprisoned (Original Content B-Plot)

**Path:** Off-Screen Development (Original Content Gap)

**Macro-Objective:**
Explore the strain of Zuko's obsession on the crew and establish Jake as the person maintaining stability aboard the ship, ending with a new canon lead.

**B-Plot Anchor:**
- The Gaang is dealing with Haru and the offshore prison rig (off-screen).
- **Ship-side Reality:** Zuko pushes the crew beyond their limits chasing the Avatar's trail across the Earth Kingdom coast. The fatigue from Chapter 5 has evolved into active resentment among the lower decks.
- **Jake's Role:** Jake manages injuries, mediates conflicts, and prevents the crew's frustration from escalating into mutiny. He covers for exhausted crewmen and acts as the buffer between Zuko's impossible orders and the crew's physical limits.
- **Drafting Note:** Seed specific crew members (names, small loyalties, e.g., a cook or a deckhand Jake helps) rather than treating them as a faceless mass. This emotional investment pays off in Chapter 18 when the crew is commandeered.
- **The Canon Hinge:** The chapter ends with the ship arriving at the abandoned, smoking metal rig in the breakout's aftermath. Zuko walks the deck, finds Katara's lost necklace with its torn fabric strap, and looks out toward the horizon with a blank, determined expression.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- **Name collision warning:** the canon earthbender boy in this episode is named Haru. He is NOT Jake's adoptive father Haru and must never be conflated with him. Prose should avoid dwelling on the name to prevent reader confusion.
- Zuko and Jake know only that the Avatar was here and escaped with earthbender prisoners.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- **Item gained (Zuko):** Katara's mother's necklace, torn strap.
- Crew state: strained but held together by Jake.

# Chapter 7: Winter Solstice, Part 1 (Episode 7)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
Show Zuko's dependence on Jake and Iroh, and establish the danger surrounding their pursuit in the Earth Kingdom.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Iroh, relaxing in a self-heated pool, is captured by Earth Kingdom soldiers who recognize the former general. They march him toward Ba Sing Se — the city he besieged for six hundred days — intending to crush his hands. Iroh leaves a trail for Zuko (his sandal).
- Zuko deduces the capture from earthbent terrain, tracks them, and rescues Iroh — kicking aside the rock about to crush Iroh's hands and breaking his chains ("Excellent form, Prince Zuko." / "You taught me well."). Together they defeat the soldiers. Zuko: "Now would you please put on some clothes?"

**Jake Integration:**
- Jake assists Zuko in tracking and rescuing Iroh. The sandal trail and the earthbent-terrain deduction are Zuko's; Jake's role is practical support and defensive cover in the fight — and private relief. Iroh's safety is one of Jake's own quiet priorities.
- **Jake can use waterbending against the Earth Kingdom soldiers** — they are non-Fire-Nation combatants with low risk of reporting back to Fire Nation command. However, bending should be subtle and defensive-focused (Flowing Guard) to minimize distinctive waterbending signatures.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- The Gaang's spirit world plotline (Hei Bai) happens entirely off-screen.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Iroh rescued safely.

# Chapter 8: Winter Solstice, Part 2 (Episode 8)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
Escalate the political danger of the hunt and establish Zuko as a wanted man by Zhao.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Zuko pursues Appa toward the Fire Sages' temple on a crescent-shaped island, despite Iroh's warning that entering Fire Nation waters risks arrest — Zuko is banished.
- Zhao commands a blockade, recognizes both targets, and orders fire on the Avatar and on Zuko, whom he calls a traitor. Zuko's ship is hit (engines damaged) but he refuses to stop. Zhao then deliberately lets Zuko pass, intending to follow him to the Avatar.
- Zuko decoys Zhao with the ship's smoke trail and slips away in a smaller vessel.
- At the temple: Zuko appears from behind a pillar restraining Aang and orders the sages to seal the sanctuary door; Aang escapes into the chamber anyway. Zhao arrives with soldiers and arrests Zuko, content to wait for the Avatar to emerge.
- Avatar Roku manifests through Aang, turns the attackers' fire back on them, and melts the chains of the captives — including Zuko, who escapes in the chaos. The temple collapses into magma. Zhao is left with five Fire Sage "traitors" in custody. Zuko watches the Avatar fly away from the deck of his ship.

**Jake Integration:**
- During the blockade run, Jake is on damage control aboard the hit ship (smoke, fire, injured men) — he cannot stop catapult stones, and the ship's survival is a crew effort. When Zuko splits off in the smaller vessel, Jake goes with him.
- At the Fire Temple, Jake remains outside securing their escape route while Zuko pursues Aang inside — consistent with his role of protection and support.
- **Emotional Beat:** Jake hears Zhao call Zuko a traitor and witnesses the arrest order. The word lands personally: the Fire Nation calls Zuko a traitor for existing; Jake is the concealed waterbender aboard his ship. The political reality of their exile is hardening.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- Jake does not enter the sanctuary and does not learn what Roku told Aang (Sozin's Comet). 
- Zhao's attention is on Zuko and the Avatar; Jake remains beneath his notice here.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Ship engines damaged (again); repaired in passing.
- Zuko is now formally wanted: Zhao ordered his arrest. "Traitor" status inside the Fire Nation military begins here.

# Chapter 9: The Waterbending Scroll (Episode 9)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
Force Jake to confront the existence of the culture and ability he lost — and execute the locked reveal: Katara witnesses Jake's waterbending.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Ship-side: Zuko spars with Lieutenant Jee; Iroh diverts the ship to a port to replace a lost white lotus tile for Pai Sho; Zuko vents his rage in smoke and flame on the bridge.
- At the port: Iroh finds no tile but buys armfuls of trinkets and instruments for shipboard music night.
- Zuko overhears two pirates describing their quarry — a bald monk and a Water Tribe girl — and whirls on them, demanding to know if the boy had an arrow on his head.
- Zuko and the pirates ambush Katara; she is tied to a tree; Zuko reveals her mother's betrothal necklace (found in Chapter 6) and offers it in trade for the Avatar; she refuses; Zuko holds a flame beneath the scroll to force the pirates to keep hunting.
- Sokka and Aang are captured; at the river shore, the scroll-for-Avatar trade collapses when Sokka points out the Avatar's black-market value. Zuko and his soldiers attack the pirates.
- The Gaang escapes on the pirates' boat; the pirates steal Zuko's smaller boat in turn; a waterfall chase; Appa saves the Gaang. Iroh's "lost" tile was in his sleeve; Zuko hurls it over the falls.

**Jake Integration:**
- Present through the port, ambush, and river-shore sequences; coordinates the soldiers during the ambush and the brawl.
- **The Reveal (Locked):** During the river-shore brawl, with the Gaang escaping and pirates between them, Jake bends openly — the river is at hand, Flowing Guard, defensive — to keep pirates off Zuko's flank. Katara, mid-escape aboard the stolen boat, sees a boy in Fire Nation red waterbend. Mutual recognition in the same instant: Jake watches Katara bend the ship free.
- No contact, no dialogue, no names exchanged. A single witnessed moment in chaos, at distance.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- Katara learns ONLY that a waterbender fights beside Zuko. She does **not** learn his name, his origin, or anything else. His Southern Water Tribe identity remains hidden.
- Jake does not learn her name; the Gaang is still unnamed to the exile side.
- The exile crew already knows Jake bends (locked) — no ship-side secrecy problem in this fight.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Reveal state: Katara knows a waterbender fights beside Zuko (origin hidden).
- Jake's first open bending before an outside witness since exile.
- The pirates are now enemies of Zuko as well (they return in Chapter 18).

# Chapter 12: The Storm (Episode 12)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**
Parallel Zuko and Jake's shared trauma around the project's foundational scene, while deepening the crew's understanding of their bond — origin layer protected.

**Canon Anchor:**
- The storm rises; Lieutenant Jee and the crew grumble about Zuko's obsession; Zuko overhears insubordination; confrontation on deck; Iroh defuses it.
- That night, in the heart of the storm, **Iroh tells the crew Zuko's story**: the war-room meeting, the general's plan to sacrifice recruits, Zuko speaking out, the challenge — discovering his opponent was his own father, refusing to fight, the scar, banishment, the quest.
- Storm climax: the helmsman is imperiled; **Zuko climbs and saves him**; lightning threatens the ship; **Iroh redirects a lightning strike**; Appa rises past the ship in the chaos and **Aang and Zuko lock eyes** across the storm before the Gaang is gone.

**Jake Integration:**
- Works the storm openly: the crew already knows he bends, so this is the one environment where Jake can waterbend without a mask — calming water across the deck, clearing swamped sections, anchoring loose gear. Unshowy, practical, relentless.
- Zuko's canon saves stay his (the helmsman). Jake covers parallel beats Zuko cannot reach — a snapped line, a flooded compartment.
- **The AU Flashback:** Iroh's storm-side narration is the natural slot for the project's foundational scene — the chapter should render the Agni Kai as a full flashback for the reader: Ozai's punishment, Jake's intervention, the first public waterbending, the burned hands.
- **Framing rule (Locked):** Iroh's spoken narration to the crew covers ZUKO'S layer and the *emotional history* of Jake's bond with Zuko. Jake's layer (the intervention, the bending, the burns, Iroh's maneuver) is rendered as Jake's own internal memory.
- **Azula Guardrail:** In the flashback audience, Azula looks intrigued/suspicious by Jake's intervention, but she does *not* instantly expose him or shout that he is a waterbender.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- Iroh does **not** expose Jake's Southern Water Tribe origin to the crew. The crew learns the emotional depth of the bond, but the SWT origin stays locked.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Relationship texture: the crew's understanding of the Jake/Zuko bond deepens.

# Chapter 13: The Blue Spirit (Episode 13)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration — Locked Major Divergence (complementary masked roles infiltrate together)

**Macro-Objective:**
Introduce the parallel identities of Blue Spirit and White Heron, showcase their seamless non-verbal combat synergy, and force Aang to witness the desperate loyalty between his pursuers.

**Canon Anchor (Modified for AU):**
- Zhao is promoted to admiral; the Yuyan archers capture Aang; Aang is imprisoned in Pohuai Stronghold.
- Zuko infiltrates alone, masked as the Blue Spirit.
- *AU Shift:* He is joined by a second masked figure. Together, they free Aang.
- The three-wall escape sequence; Zhao watches through a telescope and orders the gates held.
- At the last wall: Zhao allows them to pass. Zuko takes Aang hostage with his dual swords.
- Zhao orders **two arrows** instead of one.
- **The Blue Spirit is knocked unconscious; the mask is knocked aside; Aang sees Zuko's face.**
- Aang helps them escape under the cover of a dust screen.
- Aftermath: In the forest, Aang has a quiet moment with them before leaving; Zuko and his companion return to the ship.

**Jake Integration (White Heron):**
- Zuko sneaks out of the ship alone. Jake notices his absence, secretly takes the White Heron mask (and the Heron's bill with it) from Iroh's forgotten prop set, and follows him. Iroh already **named the mask to Jake in a quiet shipboard scene during Chapters 11–12** (locked ruling — see `Story_Bible.md`); on this night he takes it himself while Iroh sleeps. **Iroh remains completely unaware that either boy has left the ship.**
- **The Infiltration:** They operate in perfect, silent tandem. Blue Spirit provides the aggressive, lethal threat; White Heron provides silent takedowns, utility, and defense (the Heron's bill, debuting here with the mask). 
- **The Double Arrow (Locked Injury):** At the final gate, when Zhao's archer fires, one arrow knocks out Zuko. Jake throws himself into the line of fire to shield Zuko's fallen body, taking a severe, debilitating hit from the second arrow.
- **The Recognition Beat:** A bleeding, struggling White Heron ignores Aang entirely, desperately trying to drag Zuko to safety alone. Hearing a familiar pained gasp and catching sight of Jake's heavily scarred hands, Aang realizes this is the boy from Zuko's ship. Aang steps in to help the injured White Heron carry Zuko out.
- Jake, despite his severe injuries, manages to extract Zuko back to the ship. 

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- White Heron never openly waterbends.
- **Aang's Knowledge:** Aang learns Zuko is the Blue Spirit. Aang realizes the White Heron is Zuko's companion (Jake). Aang does **not** witness him bending here. 
- Zhao registers a **second masked figure** at Pohuai.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Jake seriously injured (recovery in progress).
- White Heron identity created; mask hidden aboard the ship.
- Aang now knows the identities of both masked figures.

# Chapter 15: Bato of the Water Tribe (Episode 15)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**  
Increase the danger of Jake's hidden identity being discovered by introducing his first adult Southern Water Tribe pressure point, alongside the physical threat of June and the shirshu.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Aboard the ship, June and her shirshu (Nyla) are introduced. The beast tracks a stowaway by scent.
- Zuko hires June to track Katara using her lost necklace, hoping it leads to the Avatar.
- Zuko, June, and Iroh ambush the Gaang and Bato at an abbey. A chaotic fight ensues. 
- The shirshu tracks Katara's necklace to Sokka and Katara, paralyzes them, finds the hidden map, and uses Aang's scent on it to track him back to the abbey.
- Sokka directs the nuns to dump vats of perfume, and Katara waterbends it into a mist, blinding the scent-dependent shirshu. The beast paralyzes Zuko and June in the confusion.
- Aang recovers Katara's necklace from Zuko and returns it to her as they escape.

**Jake Integration:**
- Jake fights defensively alongside Zuko at the abbey. **He is still favoring his recovering injury from Chapter 13**, forcing him to rely heavily on Flowing Guard redirection rather than raw physical movement.
- **The Threat:** The shirshu tracks Katara's necklace and Aang's map, *not* Jake (a boy raised in the Fire Nation for 15 years would not smell like the South Pole). The real danger for Jake is twofold:

# Chapter 20: The Siege of the North, Part 2 (Episode 20)

**Path:** Core Canon Integration

**Macro-Objective:**  
The climax of Book 1. Jake chooses Zuko over the spiritual crisis of his birthplace, and Zuko realizes the depth of his protective rage.

**Canon Anchor:**
- Katara, Sokka, and Yue track Zuko to an ice cave. Katara ambushes Zuko, easily defeating and freezing him.
- Aang's spirit returns to his body. As the Gaang prepares to leave Zuko to freeze, Aang shows mercy, insisting they bring the unconscious Zuko with them.
- Zhao kills the Moon Spirit (Tui). The sky goes dark; waterbenders lose their power.
- Aang merges with the Ocean Spirit (La) and decimates the Fire Nation fleet. Yue sacrifices her life to become the new Moon Spirit.
- Zuko wakes up and duels Zhao. The Ocean Spirit grabs Zhao. Zuko offers his hand to save him, but Zhao refuses out of pride and is pulled underwater.
- Zuko and Iroh escape the North Pole on a small catamaran.

**Jake Integration:**
- **Positioning Constraint (The Cave):** When Katara ambushes the cave, Jake must be incapacitated or pinned down *before* she reaches Zuko. This preserves Katara's decisive, rapid victory over Zuko, prevents her from being overpowered by fighting two benders, and ensures Aang's canon mercy beat (choosing to save Zuko) remains Aang's choice, witnessed by Jake.
- **The Moon's Death (Locked Emotional Beat):** When Zhao kills the Moon Spirit, all waterbenders instantly lose their connection to the element. Jake, already exhausted by the cold, physically weakens or collapses as his spiritual connection to water is violently severed. **Zuko witnesses this.** Zuko's subsequent duel against Zhao is fueled not just by pride or the assassination attempt, but by visceral fury that Zhao's actions directly harmed and weakened Jake.
- During the spiritual chaos (the Ocean Spirit's rampage), Jake chooses to secure Zuko's escape rather than return to defend the Water Tribe. It is his final realization that his family is the people who chose him, not the place he was born.
- Jake escapes on the catamaran with Zuko and Iroh. The final denouement features three people, not two, visually cementing their new fugitive family unit.

**Locks & Knowledge Limits:**
- Aang's mercy beat must stay Aang's.
- Yue's sacrifice remains untouched.
- Katara's victory at the cave must remain canonical.

**Tracker Seeds:**
- Book 1 ends.
- Zuko, Jake, and Iroh are fugitives on a small catamaran.
- No POV coda: the Azula coda is cut for the Book 1 restart (per `Book_1_Chapter_Index.md` and the POV record in `base/Style_Guide.md`). Azula's awareness grows off-page.
