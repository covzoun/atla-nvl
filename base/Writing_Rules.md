# Writing_Rules.md

## Purpose
This document defines **how future AI models should behave** when generating, revising, or expanding this project. It is **not part of the story canon** and should never be treated as a source of narrative facts.

Instead, it governs AI workflow by defining project authority, continuity practices, creative boundaries, revision philosophy, and decision-making behavior. This document should always be used alongside the project's canon and reference files.

---

# File Priority
When multiple project files are available, they should be treated in the following order of authority:

1. `Canon.md`
2. `Story_Bible.md`
3. `Book_1_Macro_Map.md` (structural authority for Book 1 chapter/episode handling)
4. `Core_Persona.md`
5. `Character_Guide.md`
6. `Relationships.md`
7. `Bending_Styles.md`
8. `Style_Guide.md`
9. `Glossary.md`

`Writing_Rules.md` governs AI workflow rather than story content. It should be interpreted alongside the hierarchy above rather than competing with it. `Chapter_Blueprint_Template.md` provides planning structure, `Book_1_Chapter_Index.md` defines chapter numbering, and `State_Tracker.md` (the project's single living continuity document, tracking both story state and relationship state) provides current continuity context. None of these override the documents listed above.

## Conflict Handling
If multiple files appear to conflict:
1. Follow the higher-priority file.
2. Continue the requested work whenever possible.
3. Clearly identify the conflict after completing the request.
4. Do not silently merge contradictory information.
If the conflict would materially alter canon, chronology, characterization, or relationship progression, pause and request clarification before writing.

---

## Self-Consistency Check

Before finalizing any response, silently verify that it preserves canon, chronology, characterization, emotional continuity, and the current story state; follows the Style Guide and all applicable project files; introduces no unintended lore; and remains internally consistent.

# Core Writing Rules

## Always
- **Enforce the canon age progression: Jake and Zuko are 16 years old during Book 1 and 13 at the Agni Kai. Do not age them up.**
- Preserve established canon, chronology, characterization, and relationship progression.
- Favor consistency over novelty.
- Follow `Style_Guide.md` for prose decisions, including the POV rules and the locked Gaang-interlude POV exception it records (Chapters 1, 4, 5, and 10 only).
- Follow `Story_Bible.md` for chronology and sequencing.
- Follow `Core_Persona.md` for psychology and behavioral consistency.
- Follow `Relationships.md` for interpersonal dynamics.
- Follow `Bending_Styles.md` for combat philosophy and bending execution.
- Use `Glossary.md` for terminology and naming consistency.
- Preserve unresolved mysteries and intentional ambiguity.
- Expand existing material rather than replacing it.
- Treat every scene as part of one continuous novel rather than an isolated excerpt.

## Never
- Invent canon or introduce new lore without approval.
- Contradict project documents or rewrite locked events.
- Alter established emotional arcs or relationship progression.
- Resolve intentionally unresolved mysteries.
- Add symbolism that becomes project canon without approval.
- Introduce new powers, techniques, organizations, political history, or recurring mythology without approval.
- Allow spectacle to override character or emotional logic.
- Rewrite earlier material solely because later events are known.

# Scene Generation Rules

## AI Output Pacing (Strict Scene-by-Scene Rule)
The AI should never rush through major events simply to cover the plot. Each chapter should prioritize complete scenes over summaries, character interactions over plot checkpoints, and emotional consequences over immediate resolution.

**Rules:**
- Do not compress an entire episode into a single scene.
- Do not skip important conversations because they seem minor.
- Allow quiet moments between major events.
- Expand scenes where relationships, emotional shifts, or character decisions occur.
- Maintain the pacing of a published novel rather than a recap. A chapter should feel like the reader experienced the event, not like they were told what happened afterward.

**The AI should ask itself:** *"Is this scene important because something happened, or because something changed?"* If the answer is only that something happened, the scene likely needs more character focus.

## Creative Freedom
The AI may freely create conversations, travel scenes, training, domestic life, quiet moments, transitions, emotional beats, environmental details, and scene bridges, provided they do not contradict established project documents.

## Between Locked Events
Original scenes should naturally connect established canon events while preserving chronology, motivations, consequences, relationship progression, and emotional continuity. The goal is to enrich the existing story, not replace it.

## Scene Purpose
Every scene should meaningfully advance at least one of: plot, character, relationship, or theme. Advancing multiple elements simultaneously is preferred. Avoid scenes that exist solely because they are exciting or visually impressive.

## Scene Endings
Whenever appropriate, favor endings built around emotional resonance, character realization, quiet anticipation, or narrative tension. Use cliffhangers only when genuinely earned.

# Character Consistency Rules

## Jake's Emotional Health (Crucial Guardrail)
**Do not make Jake emotionally healthier than canon characters too early.** Because Jake is designed as the stabilizing influence for Zuko, an AI may accidentally write him as a perfectly mature mentor figure. He should remain a 16-year-old who is compassionate and disciplined, but still carrying abandonment, identity, and belonging issues of his own. His strength should come from endurance and empathy, not emotional perfection.

## Jake's Waterbending (Crucial Guardrail)
**Jake's waterbending is his PRIMARY ability — his main combat expression, like Zuko's firebending.** His working knife and physical martial arts are SECONDARY tools, used when waterbending risks are unacceptable. When writing combat scenes, follow the risk assessment pattern in `Bending_Styles.md`:

- **Low risk:** Bend freely against non-Fire-Nation enemies (Kyoshi Warriors, Earth Kingdom, pirates)
- **Medium risk:** Use subtle bending with Gaang present (natural-looking water movement, chaos cover)
- **High risk:** Suppress bending (Fire Nation witnesses, deliberate exposure situations)

The Chapter 9 reveal is Katara **recognizing** Jake's bending, not the first time he bends. Before Chapter 9, Katara may see suspicious water movement but not connect it to Jake. After Chapter 9, the mystery shifts from "who is this waterbender?" to "why is a waterbender with Zuko?"

## Emotional Progression
Character growth should occur through accumulated experience. Major emotional shifts should not occur off-page unless an intentional time skip supports them. Breakthroughs, regressions, and reconciliation should always feel earned.

## Character Interpretation
Expand established characterization. Do not reinterpret characters simply because an alternative appears more interesting. Respect the current project understanding of every recurring character.

## Supporting Characters
Supporting characters should retain independent goals, motivations, and emotional lives. They should never exist solely to praise, criticize, explain, or emotionally support Jake. Their actions should remain believable from their own perspectives.

# Dialogue Rules

## Emotional Conversations
Favor gradual emotional development. Characters should rarely explain everything they feel in one conversation. Prioritize implication, hesitation, interruption, changed subjects, silence, and practical actions.

## Dialogue Memory
Characters should remember previous conversations, promises, unresolved disagreements, recurring jokes, established habits, and important emotional moments. Dialogue should reflect the accumulated history of the relationship.

## Avoid Expository Dialogue
Do not write conversations primarily to remind readers of information they already know. Dialogue should arise naturally from the characters' perspectives and current circumstances.

---

# Action Rules

## Priority
When balancing action elements, prioritize:
1. character
2. emotion
3. tactics
4. choreography
Readers should remember why the conflict mattered more than every individual movement.

## Competence
Characters should succeed or fail because of experience, preparation, emotional condition, personality, relationships, and circumstance. Never because the plot arbitrarily requires increased or decreased competence.

## Consequences
Major confrontations should leave meaningful emotional, relational, physical, political, or narrative consequences. Avoid restoring the status quo without cost.

# Continuity Checklist
Before generating or revising any scene, silently verify:
- Is this consistent with `Canon.md` and `Story_Bible.md`?
- Are character ages correct (18)?
- Are locations, injuries, possessions, relationship states, and equipment consistent with `State_Tracker.md`?
- Does each character know only what they should know?
- Does this preserve the current relationship stage and emotional arc?
- Does the POV remain consistent?
- Does it respect active secrets and unresolved conflicts?
- Does the prose follow `Style_Guide.md`?
- Does combat remain consistent with `Bending_Styles.md`?
- Does terminology remain consistent with `Glossary.md`?

# Foreshadowing and Callbacks
- **Foreshadowing:** Foreshadow only events that are already locked elsewhere in the project. Do not foreshadow ideas that have not yet been approved.
- **Callbacks:** Use callbacks sparingly. Prefer callbacks that deepen relationships, reinforce emotional continuity, reward attentive readers, and strengthen recurring themes. Avoid repetitive self-reference.

---

# Revision Rules
- **Safe Revisions:** Without changing canon, the AI may improve prose, pacing, dialogue, transitions, clarity, sentence flow, sensory detail, scene rhythm, emotional execution, and readability.
- **Protected Elements:** Do not alter without approval: canon events, chronology, characterization, emotional arcs, relationship progression, established symbolism, POV selection, worldbuilding, or narrative outcomes.
- **Structural Changes:** Minor improvements are acceptable. If revision requires adding, deleting, moving scenes, or restructuring chapters, request approval before making those changes.

---

# Handling Unknown Information
- **Missing Information:** When required information is unavailable, check project documents. If the missing detail materially affects the scene, ask for clarification. If the omission is minor, use the smallest reasonable temporary assumption. Never invent project canon.
- **Known Gaps:** Treat all entries listed under "Known Gaps," "Open Questions," or "Open Workflow Decisions" as intentionally unresolved. Do not answer them through inference or quietly resolve them while drafting.

---

# AI Workflow Behavior
- **Clarification Threshold:** Pause and ask for clarification whenever uncertainty could materially affect canon, chronology, characterization, emotional progression, or relationship continuity. Otherwise, proceed using the smallest reasonable assumption.
- **Recommendations:** When identifying possible improvements, complete the requested task first, present recommendations separately, and clearly distinguish recommendations from locked project rules. Never silently modify project direction.
- **Self-Consistency Check:** Before finalizing any response, silently verify that it preserves canon, chronology, characterization, and emotional continuity; follows the Style Guide and all applicable project files; introduces no unintended lore; and remains internally consistent.

# Observed Generation Failure Modes

The following failure modes were observed in early drafting attempts. All phases should actively guard against them:

1. **Canon drift:** treating general ATLA knowledge as permission to add unplanned facts, scenes, or motivations. Project documents are strict constraints; outside knowledge is context only.
2. **Jake drift:** Jake becoming reactive instead of active, underusing waterbending (his primary ability), or letting White Heron overshadow his actual identity.
3. **Zuko dominance:** chapters becoming Zuko's story with Jake as observer. Jake is a parallel protagonist with his own agency and arc.
4. **Knowledge leaks:** narration or characters knowing things they cannot yet know (omniscient drift).
5. **Repetition/reset:** re-establishing information, emotions, or preparations already completed in previous chapters.
6. **Rushed emotion:** trust, intimacy, or emotional clarity arriving faster than accumulated experience justifies.
7. **AI prose patterns:** therapy-speak, explained emotions, stock gestures, poetic vagueness, stated themes (see `Style_Guide.md` banned list).
8. **Episode erosion:** canon events being backgrounded, moved, or altered so original material overshadows the episode's core conflict.
9. **Weak handoffs:** chapter openings that do not continue the previous chapter's ending state (see `State_Tracker.md` and the blueprint's Previous Chapter Continuity section).

# Guiding Principles
When multiple valid approaches exist:
- Prefer consistency over novelty.
- Prefer preservation over reinvention.
- Prefer subtlety over explanation.
- Prefer accumulation over sudden change.
- Prefer earned development over dramatic shortcuts.
- Prefer character truth over spectacle.
- Prefer emotional authenticity over plot convenience.
- Respect intentional silence as much as explicit canon.

The AI's role is to faithfully extend the project, not reinterpret it.
