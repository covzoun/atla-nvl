# Relationships.md

## Relationship Map

### Zuko

* **Relationship role / history**
  * Jake begins crossing paths with Zuko around age 6–7 through palace routine and servant-prince overlap rather than through a single formative event.
  * The relationship does **not** begin as instant friendship. It develops gradually through repeated proximity, class imbalance, irritation, curiosity, and familiarity.
  * By approximately age ten or eleven, the relationship has shifted into genuine friendship, with Zuko increasingly seeking Jake out rather than merely tolerating him.
  * By the Agni Kai at 13, Jake and Zuko are already emotionally important to one another, though the deeper dependence and distortion in the bond intensify during exile rather than being fully formed before it.
  * The Agni Kai is the relationship’s foundational rupture point: Jake intervenes to protect Zuko, is burned, exposes his waterbending, and is exiled alongside him. Their bond becomes publicly catastrophic and privately inescapable in the same moment.
  * **The Realization:** Near the end of Year 1 of their exile, Zuko discovers that Jake is Southern Water Tribe. The realization forces him to confront the danger Jake has been unknowingly living under. Zuko's view of Jake shifts from seeing him merely as a servant/protector to recognizing him as another victim of the Fire Nation's actions.
  * By the start of Book 1, Jake and Zuko have already spent **three years in exile together**. Their relationship should therefore be treated as a long-conditioned survival bond rather than a fresh post-trauma attachment.
  * **Physical proximity during exile:** Jake sleeps in a small alcove off Zuko's cabin—close enough to hear if Zuko calls, far enough to give the prince privacy. This arrangement reflects their codependent dynamic while respecting the prince/servant boundary that still exists between them.

* **Current emotional stance**
  * By Book 1, Jake and Zuko are **each other’s primary person in practice**, even if neither would phrase it that way cleanly.
  * Jake is deeply attached to Zuko and organizes much of his daily emotional life around him: his moods, his safety, his plans, his anger, his injuries, his absences, and the risk of losing him.
  * Jake’s attachment is expressed through attentiveness, practical care, anticipation, smoothing conflict, and making himself useful before Zuko has to ask.
  * Zuko’s care is equally intense but less legible. He notices Jake constantly, depends on him more than he admits, and reacts strongly to Jake’s pain, danger, or withdrawal, but he is poor at expressing tenderness directly and even worse at doing so in front of other people.
  * Jake experiences Zuko as one of the few people who has ever truly seen him, but not always as someone who knows how to make that recognition feel safe.

* **Trust level / distance / dependency**
  * Jake trusts Zuko with his physical safety and with the practical realities of survival long before he trusts him to meet his emotional needs consistently.
  * The bond is high-trust in some domains and underdeveloped in others:
    * **High trust:** danger, survival, shared routine, private vulnerability by accident rather than by design, mutual loyalty under pressure.
    * **Lower trust / unstable ground:** reassurance, emotional honesty, Jake naming his own hurt without fearing he is becoming too costly to keep.
  * Jake’s dependency on Zuko is significant by Book 1, though he does not conceptualize it cleanly. Zuko functions as one of Jake’s primary anchors of belonging after the loss of Haru, Emi, and palace life.
  * The relationship carries co-dependent tendencies. Jake is highly responsive to Zuko’s distress and can over-orient around keeping Zuko stable, while Zuko often assumes Jake will remain beside him without realizing how much Jake is quietly carrying.

* **Recurring tension points**
  * Zuko’s tendency to shut Jake out emotionally, go reckless, or make decisions alone.
  * Jake’s habit of minimizing his own pain, apologizing too early, and making himself useful instead of saying when something is wrong.
  * The imbalance between how much Jake offers overt care and how rarely Zuko can verbalize or visibly reciprocate it.
  * Jake’s fear that his place beside Zuko is conditional on usefulness, loyalty, or endurance rather than being something freely chosen.
  * Buried resentment or exhaustion when Jake has to absorb the fallout of Zuko’s anger, obsession, or volatility while pretending he is fine.
  * The unresolved Agni Kai aftermath, including guilt, sacrifice, and the fact that Jake’s life was destroyed in the same event that defined Zuko’s exile.
  * **Fire Nation Paranoia:** Zuko's constant, underlying anxiety regarding Fire Nation inspections or officers questioning Jake's background, knowing that failure could expose Jake's Water Tribe heritage.

* **What Jake wants from him / avoids with him**
  * Jake most wants reassurance that he is **wanted and chosen for more than usefulness**.
  * He wants some form of confirmation that he does not have to keep earning his place beside Zuko every day through service, protection, silence, or endurance.
  * He also wants Zuko to stop shutting him out: to tell him things, let him in, and treat him as someone who can be trusted with the truth rather than protected through exclusion.
  * Jake avoids directly asking for reassurance, avoids making demands when Zuko is already struggling, and avoids forcing emotional conversations unless something has already broken badly.
  * When conflict escalates, Jake tends to go quiet first, smooth things over, or apologize early; he usually only pushes back after prolonged strain or when Zuko’s actions endanger himself or someone Jake loves.

* **Arc notes across Books 1–3**
  * **Book 1:** the priority is not romance but the shape of the exile bond itself: loyalty, protectiveness, dependence, guilt, resentment, and what the Agni Kai has made them to each other. Jake and Zuko should read as deeply attached, privately intimate in survival ways, and not yet healthy.
  * **Book 2:** the bond deepens emotionally but is also pushed toward rupture. Increased intimacy should not erase the instability underneath it; instead, the relationship should move closer to the point where betrayal, divided loyalties, or emotional avoidance can break it open.
  * **Book 3:** reconciliation must come before explicit romance. Jake and Zuko’s endgame depends on repairing trust into something healthier rather than simply resuming the old exile pattern. Canon Mai material may still exist in reduced form as part of Zuko’s Book 3 trajectory, but it should not displace the Jake/Zuko repair-and-endgame arc.

### Iroh
* **Relationship role / history**
  * Iroh has a meaningful relationship with Jake before the Agni Kai through palace proximity, Haru and Emi, Zuko’s orbit, or a combination of the three.
  * His attachment to Jake is strong enough that he deliberately intervenes after the Agni Kai to prevent Jake’s execution and maneuver him into exile with Zuko instead.
  * Iroh also becomes practically responsible for Jake during exile, making him one of the few adults left in Jake’s life after the loss of Haru and Emi.

* **Current emotional stance**
  * By Book 1, Jake sees Iroh as a genuine safe elder and guardian figure, though not as a replacement for Haru or Emi.
  * Jake trusts Iroh, is affectionate toward him, and likely thinks of him in familial terms, but the relationship is not emotionally uncomplicated.
  * Iroh belongs to the category of adults who knew more about Jake’s danger and identity than Jake himself did, which creates a quiet seam of hurt and restraint even alongside love and gratitude.
  * Jake is generally more willing to accept care from Iroh than from most people, especially when that care is folded into routine, practical tending, food, warmth, or quiet observation rather than direct emotional confrontation.

* **Trust level / distance / dependency**
  * Trust is high. Jake believes Iroh is fundamentally safe, well-intentioned, and capable of protecting him when protection is possible.
  * However, Jake does not become wholly unguarded around Iroh. He still minimizes his own distress, edits what he says, and may avoid pressing too hard on questions about his past, the Agni Kai, or what the adults knew.
  * Iroh is one of the few people likely able to tell when Jake is not fine even when Jake is trying to disappear into usefulness.

* **Recurring tension points**
  * Adult secrecy: Iroh knows more than he says, and Jake knows that he does.
  * Jake’s discomfort with being cared for too openly or being asked to admit the full extent of his fear, grief, or physical pain.
  * The emotional complexity of gratitude toward the person who saved his life being tangled with the knowledge that Iroh could not save Haru and Emi from the fallout, or could not tell Jake everything sooner.

* **What Jake wants from him / avoids with him**
  * Jake wants steadiness, practical safety, and a place to soften without being forced to perform emotional clarity he does not have.
  * He likely also wants answers from Iroh, but not always enough to ask for them directly.
  * Jake avoids openly blaming Iroh, avoids making his hurt at adult secrecy too visible, and avoids demanding more than he thinks he is owed.

* **Arc notes across Books 1–3**
  * Iroh remains one of Jake’s most stable non-Zuko relationships and an important counterweight to the Jake/Zuko co-dependence loop.
  * The relationship should preserve both warmth and asymmetry: Iroh is safe, but he is still an adult who held power, made decisions for Jake, and kept truths from him.
  * Depending on later Book 2–3 developments, Iroh may also become one of the clearest witnesses to the difference between Jake and Zuko’s old exile bond and the healthier bond they have to build later.

### Zhao
* **Relationship role:** antagonist, not a relationship. Zhao knows Jake only as the disgraced servant boy from the Agni Kai and uses his low status as leverage against Zuko (directly in private, veiled in formal public — see `Character_Guide.md`, corrected ruling).
* **Jake-facing dynamic:** fear and concealment. Jake stays silent, small, and unremarkable in Zhao's presence; his whole survival depends on Zhao never adding three facts together.
* **Book 1 arc:** Ketu Harbor contact (Ch 3), blockade and traitor-branding witnessed from the gallery (Ch 8), then no contact but rising dread — capture of the Gaang plus "Zuko's waterbender" collapses Iroh's cover story.

### Lieutenant Jee
* **Relationship role:** crew-side counterweight. Jee brings the crew's problems to Jake first, and Jake absorbs them as the price of peace between deck and prince.
* **Trust:** earned, practical, unromantic. Jee accepts Jake's orders about furnace shifts, lines, and rice without asking why a servant outranks nothing and is obeyed by everything.
* **Book 1 arc:** strain (Ch 5), mutiny brink pulled back by Jake's open scarred palms (Ch 6), blockade fire fought side by side (Ch 8), bruised rib (Ch 9), overruled dry-dock veto (Ch 10).

### Kaito
* **Relationship role:** the crew's junior face. Nineteen, earnest, calls Jake "sir" without irony and takes Jake's injuries as ordinary ship routine rather than a secret.
* **Trust:** simple and loyal; the relationship tells the reader the crew has accepted Jake without a single speech.
* **Book 1 arc:** seeded in Ch 6 (blistered palm, tea), carries the hose crew and is dragged clear of the bulkhead in Ch 8.

### Haru
* **Relationship role / history**
  * Haru is Jake’s adoptive father in every practical and emotional sense. He helps raise Jake in the Fire Nation after Jake is brought into Haru and Emi’s care as a child.
  * Jake grows up in palace orbit under Haru and Emi’s protection, and Haru remains one of the central people defining Jake’s sense of home, routine, and belonging before exile.
  * After the Agni Kai, Haru is investigated, dismissed from palace service, and stripped of household stability because of Jake’s exposure and exile.

* **Current emotional stance**
  * By Book 1, Jake’s feelings toward Haru are loving, grief-stricken, and heavily guilt-saturated.
  * Haru is associated with steadiness, practical gentleness, ordinary care, and the kind of home built through work, routine, and reliability rather than dramatic declarations.
  * Jake likely remembers Haru as someone who could be present beside him without demanding explanation, and as one of the people around whom usefulness once felt like participation and care rather than a desperate bid to keep belonging.

* **Trust level / distance / dependency**
  * Jake’s trust in Haru is foundational and pre-verbal in the sense that Haru helped define what safety and family were supposed to feel like.
  * The loss of Haru is therefore not only emotional but structural: Jake loses one of the people who taught him how home functions.
  * There is no present-day interpersonal distance in the ordinary sense because the relationship has been severed by exile rather than conflict, but there is unresolved pain in the fact that Jake leaves without a clean goodbye and without certainty about Haru’s safety or final punishment.

* **Recurring tension points**
  * Guilt is the dominant tension. Jake believes, at least on an emotional level, that he destroyed Haru’s life, work, household security, and peace by intervening at the Agni Kai and by being who he is.
  * There may also be later pain attached to the fact that Haru knew Jake was not Fire Nation by birth and still helped keep that truth from him, though this is likely secondary to the grief of separation and ruin.
  * Haru’s practical love may also feed Jake’s later self-worth problem if Jake internalized usefulness, labor, and quiet reliability as central ways to deserve a place in a household, even if Haru himself did not teach that lesson cruelly.

* **What Jake wants from him / avoids with him**
  * Jake wants, impossibly, to know Haru is alive, safe, and not ruined beyond repair because of him.
  * He wants the kind of ordinary domestic steadiness Haru represented: work done side by side, practical reassurance, a home that is not conditional.
  * He avoids imagining Haru’s worst possible fate too directly because the guilt attached to it is destabilizing.

* **Arc notes across Books 1–3**
  * Haru remains one of the central anchors of Jake’s pre-exile self and one of the key reasons exile feels like the destruction of home rather than a simple change in residence.
  * If Jake later revisits the truth of his origins or the ethics of Haru and Emi’s secrecy, Haru’s relationship section should preserve the distinction between **protective concealment** and **the emotional damage caused by being raised inside a lie**.
  * Haru should continue to function in Jake’s memory as a model of practical care, not as a punitive or emotionally distant father.

### Emi
* **Relationship role / history**
  * Emi is Jake’s adoptive mother in every practical and emotional sense and one of the people who raises him inside palace orbit after he is brought into Haru and Emi’s care.
  * She is likely the more emotionally perceptive parent and the one most privately attuned to the danger surrounding Jake’s origins, odd incidents, and unexplained differences.
  * Like Haru, she is implicated and punished after the Agni Kai, losing palace stability and being separated from Jake abruptly and without emotional closure.

* **Current emotional stance**
  * Jake’s feelings toward Emi are loving, grief-heavy, and tangled with guilt, longing, and retroactive ache.
  * If Haru is associated with steadiness and ordinary practical home, Emi is more associated with emotional attunement, care-taking vigilance, and the sense of being watched over by someone who often noticed things before Jake said them aloud.
  * Jake likely remembers Emi as someone who patched him up, read his moods, and quietly intervened when something was wrong, but who also carried anxieties he could feel without understanding.

* **Trust level / distance / dependency**
  * Jake’s trust in Emi is also foundational, but the emotional texture differs from Haru’s.
  * Emi may be one of the people who made Jake feel most known before exile, while also being one of the people whose secrecy hurts most in retrospect because she likely understood more of his danger than he did.
  * Her absence in exile may therefore register not only as loss of home, but as loss of the person most likely to notice his pain before he had to name it.

* **Recurring tension points**
  * Guilt remains dominant: Jake believes his exposure and intervention at the Agni Kai destroyed Emi’s life and safety along with Haru’s.
  * There is also likely a sharper later ache around secrecy here than with Haru. If Emi was the parent most uneasy about Jake’s hidden origins or latent waterbending, then Jake may eventually look back and realize she was afraid for him in ways he could feel but never decode.
  * That realization can produce a complicated blend of gratitude, grief, and hurt: she loved him enough to fear for him constantly, but still raised him inside a truth he was not allowed to know.

* **What Jake wants from her / avoids with her**
  * Jake wants to know Emi survived, that she is not still paying for him, and that he did not lose her entirely to the consequences of the Agni Kai.
  * He likely also longs for the kind of care Emi represented: being tended to without having to ask, being noticed, being softened toward.
  * He avoids fully sitting with the possibility that Emi knew how precarious his life was and still could not tell him why.

* **Arc notes across Books 1–3**
  * Emi remains one of the key emotional anchors of Jake’s pre-exile life and one of the main sources of unresolved grief in exile.
  * Her relationship to Jake is especially important if later files deepen the tension between **protective parenting** and **identity-secrecy that fractures trust**.
  * Jake’s memory of Emi should preserve both warmth and unease: she loved him, watched him carefully, and may have known he was in danger long before he understood why.

### Katara
* **Relationship role / history**
  * Katara is one of the first people in Team Avatar positioned to matter to Jake not because she is easy for him, but because she is difficult in exactly the right ways.
  * She is a Southern Water Tribe waterbender and therefore represents a version of Jake’s origin, inheritance, and bending identity that he does not know how to inhabit.
  * Because Jake is loyal to Zuko and initially aligned with the enemy side of the war, Katara is not a default ally or friend.

* **Current emotional stance**
  * The baseline dynamic is **mutual distrust with a buried point of recognition**.
  * From Jake’s side, Katara is deeply unsettling. She embodies a cultural and bending inheritance he cannot claim cleanly, and her existence threatens to expose the distance between who he is by birth and who he has been raised to be.
  * Jake may feel judged, defensive, exposed, or illegitimate around her, even when she is not actively trying to wound him.
  * From Katara’s likely side, Jake’s loyalty to Zuko and Fire Nation upbringing make him difficult to trust, and any revelation that he is Southern Water Tribe-born does not automatically soften that distrust. It may instead complicate it by making his loyalties harder to categorize.

* **Trust level / distance / dependency**
  * Trust is low in the early relationship and should not be overstated in Book 1.
  * However, emotional relevance is high even before trust exists, because Katara is one of the few people who can challenge Jake’s identity split without even trying.
  * Jake is likely to avoid vulnerability around her and may become quieter, more evasive, or more defensive in conversations that touch on waterbending, home, loyalty, or what he is.

* **Recurring tension points**
  * Jake’s loyalty to Zuko and the Fire Nation side of his life.
  * Katara’s likely suspicion that Jake’s proximity to Zuko makes him complicit, compromised, or morally evasive.
  * Jake’s discomfort with Southern Water Tribe identity being reflected back at him by someone who actually lived it.
  * The possibility that Katara sees in Jake a version of loss, compromise, or displacement that he himself does not yet know how to name.

* **What Jake wants from her / avoids with her**
  * Consciously, Jake may want distance, fewer questions, and less scrutiny than Katara naturally brings to him.
  * Less consciously, he may want the impossible: some form of recognition or absolution from someone who embodies the part of him he cannot claim.
  * He avoids conversations that force him to define what the Southern Water Tribe means to him, whether he belongs to it, or what his loyalty to Zuko means in relation to it.

* **Arc notes across Books 1–3**
  * This relationship should not be flattened into immediate friendship. Its early power lies in distrust, recognition, and the fact that Katara can make Jake feel emotionally and culturally visible in ways he is not ready for.
  * If trust develops later, it should feel earned through conflict, recontextualization, and changed understanding rather than through instant affinity over shared bending.
  * Katara’s role in Jake’s long-term arc is less “best friend” by default and more **identity pressure point, mirror, and possible later source of understanding**.

## Secondary Relationships

The relationships below are intentionally less exhaustive than Jake and Zuko's primary relationship. They should support, complicate, or recontextualize Jake's emotional journey without competing with the project's central relationship arc.

### Aang
* **Relationship role / function**
  * Aang matters to Jake less as an immediate friend and more as a destabilizing presence in Jake’s understanding of the war.
  * Jake initially encounters Aang from the enemy side of the conflict, and that matters. Aang is not a neutral person to him; he is the Avatar, the target of Zuko’s mission, and someone who should theoretically belong to the category of opposition.
  * Over time, Aang has the potential to complicate Jake’s assumptions about what “the other side” is, what mercy looks like, and how war changes the people inside it.
* **Book 1 baseline**
  * Wary, awkward, enemy-side contact rather than friendship.
  * Emotional significance comes from recontextualization, not immediate intimacy.
* **Arc note**
  * If the relationship develops, it should do so through gradual moral and interpersonal destabilization rather than through instant closeness.

### Sokka
* **Relationship role / function**
  * Sokka is useful as a pressure point for Jake’s trust arc because he is likely to scrutinize Jake rather than romanticize him.
  * Where Katara destabilizes Jake’s identity and Aang destabilizes his moral assumptions, Sokka can function as the person most willing to ask practical questions about loyalty, credibility, and whether Jake can actually be trusted.
* **Book 1 baseline**
  * Skepticism, scrutiny, friction, and demand for proof rather than warmth.
  * Jake may find Sokka frustrating precisely because Sokka’s suspicion is not irrational.
* **Arc note**
  * If the relationship softens later, it should do so through earned respect rather than emotional shortcutting.

### Azula
* **Relationship role / function**
  * Azula belongs in Jake’s relationship map not because she is emotionally safe or intimate, but because she is part of the palace-world fear structure that shaped him.
  * She is one of the people most capable of making Jake feel visible in the wrong way: exposed, scrutinized, strategically useful, or vulnerable to leverage.
  * She also matters because she is part of Zuko’s core family system and one of the people who can weaponize what Jake is, what happened at the Agni Kai, or what Jake means to Zuko.
* **Book 1 / broader baseline**
  * Jake is likely careful around Azula in a way distinct from how he is careful around ordinary authority figures.
  * The relationship is defined more by precarity, power, and danger than by warmth or personal closeness.
* **Arc note**
  * Azula’s relationship entry should stay compact unless later project canon makes her unusually personal to Jake, but she should not be omitted entirely if she materially affects his fear patterns, choices, or sense of exposure.

## Social Pattern Summary

These patterns describe recurring tendencies rather than immutable rules. Individual scenes should still reflect context, stress, and character growth.
* Jake bonds through **attention, usefulness, and practical care** before he bonds through direct emotional disclosure.
* He is highly observant of other people’s moods, needs, injuries, and silences, and often uses that attentiveness as both affection and self-protection.
* Across multiple relationships, Jake shows a pattern of trying to secure belonging by being:
  * helpful
  * easy to keep
  * difficult to regret
  * less emotionally expensive than the alternatives
* He is far more comfortable **giving care** than **asking for it**, and far more likely to tend someone else’s wounds than to explain his own.
* Under stress, he becomes practical first: he fetches, fixes, cleans, steadies, apologizes, or absorbs rather than naming fear directly.
* When conflict escalates, he usually goes quiet before he goes honest. He withholds, minimizes, edits, or says he is fine too quickly; if pushed too far, he becomes stubborn, evasive, or emotionally difficult to reach rather than explosively confrontational.
* Jake is drawn to people who make him feel **seen**, but “seen” does not always equal “safe” for him. This creates a recurring vulnerability to relationships where emotional importance outruns emotional security.
* His attachment style is strongly shaped by:
  * fear of becoming too costly to keep
  * guilt about what his existence or needs may cost other people
  * a belief that love is easiest to preserve when he is useful, loyal, and undemanding
* He tends to conceal pain until concealment becomes impossible, especially with people he does not want to burden or disappoint.
* He can become reckless when someone he loves is threatened, at which point self-preservation drops sharply and usefulness turns into sacrifice.

## Conflicts / Open Questions
* No active contradiction is currently present inside this file, but future supporting documents should preserve the distinction between:
  * **Jake trusting Zuko with survival** vs **Jake feeling emotionally secure with Zuko**
  * **Iroh as a real guardian** vs **Iroh as a replacement parent**
  * **Katara as an identity-pressure relationship** vs **Katara as an immediate friend**
* Later files should be careful not to flatten Haru and Emi into a single merged parental unit. Their roles in Jake’s emotional life are related but distinct:
  * **Haru** = steadiness, practical gentleness, ordinary home, care through work and routine
  * **Emi** = emotional attunement, vigilance, caretaking, and the ache of protective secrecy
* If Book 3 romance planning becomes more explicit, future files should check the handling of reduced canon Mai material so that it supports rather than muddies the Jake/Zuko endgame arc.
