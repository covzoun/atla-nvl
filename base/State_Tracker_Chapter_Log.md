## Relationship History

### Jake ↔ Zuko — chapter log

Baseline: three-year exile survival bond per `Relationships.md`; each other's primary person in practice though neither names it cleanly.

- **Chapter 03** — Zuko kept Jake close as attendant rather than leaving him aboard, defended him when Zhao used him as insult-prop (triggering the Agni Kai challenge), pressed his shoulder in passing and ordered Iroh to stay close to him; Jake prepped Zuko for the duel and registered the ground-strike mercy as proof Zuko is not Ozai, deepening the bond from trauma-bond toward genuine respect.
- **Chapter 04** — sparred with real fire against defensive water in unspoken coordination, fought back-to-back at Kyoshi with Jake shielding Zuko from warriors and the Unagi wave; Zuko gruffly stopped Jake's trembling overexertion in the cabin.
- **Chapter 05** — Jake buffered Jee against Zuko twice (translating full-speed into half-speed through narrows; physically gripping burning wrists to break the cliff blast until fire died); Zuko listened only to Jake, withdrew without apology, then softened over tea and conceded engines night.
- **Chapter 06** — the gap widened: Zuko demanded the impossible turn, raged at the dropped sail rather than at near-death, returned wordless to the bridge, walked past Jake without looking with no word to anyone; Jake absorbed his own anger to stop Jee drawing, hauled the line to force the crew back to work, pledged "I'll handle the prince," and now dreads the necklace-locked pursuit with failing hands.
- **Chapter 07** — Zuko chose Iroh's tracks over the visible Appa lead (ribbon in hand) while Jake waited silent without pushing; Jake followed instantly at a run, shielded Zuko's back in the hollow without being asked, then walked level shoulder-to-shoulder with matched boot rhythm back to the ship; dread replaced by quiet resolve.
- **Chapter 08** — Jake stood at Zuko's shoulder on the bridge without a word as the blockade fired, hauled sandbags and dragged Kaito while refusing the sea under Fire Nation eyes, knelt to the davit ropes with wrecked hands as Zuko paid out the line without telling him to stay, rowed in wordless rhythm to Zuko's half-beat calls, caught Zuko at the gallery stair and pointed the counted turns out, swung them both across the magma fissure on a temple hoist rope cut free with the working knife (weight carried on his crossed forearms, Zuko holding his waist), and rowed clear beside him; Zuko saw the blood on the rope, lifted his hands half-way, said nothing, and rowed the second oar.
- **Chapter 09** — seamless unit: Zuko stepped half-pace right to shield Jake at the pirate table without knowing it; Jake took the rear flank and high ground, called "left," stepped into the blind-side sword arc with river water and held back-to-back without words; Zuko never saw the reveal or the eye-lock and passed Jake without a glance at the falls; Jake fell in half-step behind the left shoulder though it felt moved.
- **Chapter 10** — Zuko found Jake picking/bleeding in the alcove, cold-cloth cleaned and rebound the left palm with the second wrap and ordered him to bridge; vetoed the dry-dock with a table-slam before Jake could speak and covered it with the invented inland rumor; let his gaze touch Jake's bound hand then away, which Jake read as done-for-him at Jee's price; shielded Jake 5+ times in the woods, walked the camp perimeter before stopping, crossed the fire to hold Jake's wrist to stop picking, said "We're not going to any ports. Not until I say so," and kept watch while Jake slept.
- **Chapter 11** — the whole four-day bust was spent in a two-line order ("Get the anchor up. We go north.") and the prince went below and stayed there; his door lamp burned every night for a week; he refused Jee's ten-minutes-at-the-rail request as delivered through Jake's softened telling ("If he wants to stand at the rail he can come up"), then refused it outright ("Tell the lieutenant I am not coming up. And tell him to say it to me himself, if he wants to say it that badly") — spoken in the flat level voice he uses when holding something down. Jake first lied to Jee about it ("He'll see you tomorrow"), then retracted it in the morning and told him the plain truth; when Jee asked whether Zuko knows what the prince is spending, Jake answered honestly: "No. I don't think he knows."
- **Chapter 12** — Zuko refused Iroh's anchorage course change in front of the deck with no cruelty in it ("The Avatar comes first. If the storm comes, the crew pumps"); warned Jee off speaking of Iroh ("Mind how you speak of my uncle, Lieutenant"), then challenged him outright ("Then I will teach you some. Here. Now") and was separated from the fight by Iroh's noodles; took Iroh's bowl, ate standing up as fuel and no ceremony, lifted Iroh's hand off his shoulder like a rope he did not want but could not drop, and refused Iroh's help keeping order ("And I do not need your help keeping order on my ship"). At the bow rail with Jake he offered to take the pump watch, was refused with practical care, gave Jake the standing order "Wake me if the wind comes around — the wind coming around means it is looking at us," and went below. At the lightning strike he climbed the mast without three points of contact, caught the falling helmsman's wrist, and rode the fall to the mast collar so Jee could take the weight; he said nothing while the whole mast watched. In the eye his shoulders were down for the first time in three days; he apologized to Iroh ("I called it a crew matter and then I spent the crew — I'm sorry, Uncle") and let the apology be received exactly wrong so as to be exactly right. The Avatar rose out of the eye with three riders; boy and prince locked eyes across sixty feet; Zuko's hand came up an inch and went back down, and he gave the ship north: "Let him go. Not today. Set a course for safer waters, Lieutenant. And thank your crew for me."

**Secrets ledger (Jake ↔ Zuko):** none between them about Jake's identity — Zuko knows Jake is Southern Water Tribe-born and a waterbender; Jake habitually withholds his own fear, pain, and exhaustion. Chapter 06 — Jake privately recognized the torn necklace from the village-shore/ice girl and told no one why he remembered it; Chapter 07 — continued to tell no one; Chapter 08 — still untold; Chapter 09 — Zuko used the necklace openly as leverage, Jake's earlier private recognition remains untold; Chapter 10 — no new identity secrets; Zuko never names waterbending/Katara/Zhao-leak aloud, Jake never states thanks or argument, protection stays logistical and unspoken. Chapter 12 — Iroh's telling to the crew keeps Jake's layer out entirely (the Agni Kai remains Zuko's story only; the waterbender in the ring is never named; Jake does not name himself, and Iroh's closing line "A telling is a roof" is left unopened); the crew closes ranks around the omission by Jee's order ("below this deck"); the stoker boy's forbidden question (what he saw the night the ship cleared the capes and who came up the other side) is planted and shut, not answered; every new shield Jake bends under crew cover (forepeak, spar line, whip line) is clothed in rescue-and-rope-work the same hour.

### Jake ↔ Iroh — chapter log

Baseline high trust; Iroh a safe elder and guardian, not a replacement for Haru or Emi.

- **Chapter 07** — Jake checked Iroh post-rescue in the quiet; Iroh held Jake's wrist, looked at the blood-soaked gloves, answered with plain tired gratitude and grief without scolding; Jake met it with a nod and looked away first.
- **Chapter 08** — parted without argument: Iroh to keep the smoking ship heading north as Zhao's decoy, Jake to the launch with Zuko; Iroh closed one broad hand around Jake's stained-glove shoulder ("Both of you"), Jake answered "Yes," and the smoke closed between them with one hand lifted from the deck.
- **Chapter 09** — Iroh salved and rewrapped both hands, cut second wraps for tomorrow, ordered no hauling ("on loan until sundown"); staged the tile lie to force a ten-minute breathe stop; watched the shore fight from the slope, saw Jake's bending and wet sleeve, gave a closed-door nod and said nothing.
- **Chapter 10** — stood at the bridge head, hands in sleeves, no tea, eyes lowered, saying nothing at all while Zuko overrode Jee; Jake registered it as a different kind of silence.
- **Chapter 11** — Jake sat with him in the lee of the deckhouse and asked him to speak to the prince about the ship; Iroh refused with the cost arithmetic ("a boy of sixteen told the true arithmetic of his own life will take the ship, or he will take the Avatar; both of them leave this deck poorer"), and instead gave Jake the fact: "The crew has not given their loyalty to the prince. They have given it to you... Spend it on them. And he will never know that you did it." Iroh had known the coal figure since the day it was counted ("I said a great many things, and none of them were to you") and had said nothing. He then stood an hour at the rail in the prince's place — which every man on the deck knew was not the same as the prince.
- **Chapter 12** — Iroh covered for Zuko with Jee at the approval rail ("when the words are all he has left; give him as long as the storm takes"); separated Zuko and Jee at the bow with a pot of noodles ("It is impossible to fight with honor after noodles"); then told the crew Zuko's story below decks — the war councils at eleven, the recruits-division plan, the challenge, the opponent revealed as his own father, the kneeling refusal said three times, "You will learn respect and suffering will be your teacher" — Zuko's layer only: the waterbender in the ring is never named, and Jake's layer survives only as Iroh's looks and the closer "A telling is a roof." He walked into the lightning at his own ambling pace and redirected the bolt out of his other hand ("It was a small one. And I have been wanting fresh air all evening"), with one low instruction to Jake in passing: "Nothing is coming aboard tonight but weather. See to that." In the eye he received Zuko's apology on behalf of the crew, the ship, and the storm. At the rail in the morning he poured tea by name down the line and handed Jake's cup into the ruined glove, holding on longer than tea requires, and said nothing at all.

- **Chapter 09** — Zuko drove Jee to a knee with bruised rib in punishing sparring; Jake lifted him by the forearm to check; crew gave the center berth while coiling unneeded line; three soldiers in plain cloaks went to the woods; ship at port with list, grinding engines, thin brown smoke.

- **Chapter 11** — the crew fought the arithmetic instead of the sea: they did the sum aloud on the fourth night ("Five days of coal") with Jake lying in his alcove and deliberately not correcting it; Kaito polled the stokers and brought back "he wanted to sleep" as the whole list; Jake gave the stokers four hours in the middle watch and carried the pump relay himself for three nights. Jee asked Jake — not the prince — for ten minutes of the prince's face at the rail, softened it in Jake's telling, and held his complaint: "I have my answer, and I will hold it, and I will keep it below this deck with the rest of us. The complaint is not about the money." The hulls were watched and collectively unmentioned; the gulls went wrong (low, inland, in one direction, still in sight) and the glass fell, and the log got the weather and one unwritable column of coal. - **Chapter 12** — the storm re-anchored morale: the forepeak stokers took up station on Jake's off side and matched his bending bucket for bucket without a comment; the watch hauled Jake's offered spar line and "credited the rope with an opinion"; the crew crowded the coal stove below decks while Iroh told the story, and Jee closed it — "That's where it stays. Below this deck" — making the omission the crew's to keep. In the eye Jee ran the sound-off and got "Forepeak dry, sir" from Jake; Kaito laughed once at nothing; Zuko caught the helmsman and Jee carried him down. At safer waters Jee put his fist over his own heart and made himself busy with the blankets as if the gesture had not happened. In the aftermath the stoker boy started the forbidden question ("I saw somebody go over the rail into the ring, and I saw who came up the other—") and Jee closed it with rope work ("Get that gasket line dried before it takes a set"); Kaito's "Tea's hot below" put the ship back into routine.
