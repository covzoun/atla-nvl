# Part 1 — Jake's hands

## 1.1 What the draft currently does

The revision plan already counted raw hand and palm references per chapter: **25, 29, 38, 20, 56, 75, 56, 69, 75, 48**. Six of ten chapters open on Jake's hands, a bandage, or a body inventory. The plan's conclusion was that the motif has become the main event.

The injury chain itself:

| Ch | Hand beat | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Old scars ache under the gloves at the harbour | Baseline |
| 5 | Grips Zuko's fire-wreathed wrists; gloves heat through; palms red, tremor | **Zuko's fire** |
| 5 | Basin scene: water drawn up to the scars, sting and heat ease | First-waterbending habit |
| 6 | Hemp work splits both palms; removes gloves in the mess; "the palm was a ruin" | Rope work |
| 7 | Splits pulse while walking; Iroh's eyes go to the gloves | Consequence, untreated |
| 8 | Left palm splits through the wrap; wrap black with blood; fissure swing bursts the seam | Rope + weight |
| 9 | Iroh salves and rewraps; during the fight the linen tears and the palm bleeds again | New damage |
| 10 | Picking relapse; left palm reopened and bleeding; Zuko cleans and rebinds; glove pinned | Stress + neglect |

That is six consecutive chapters of acute escalation, plus a new tear in Chapter 9 and another reopening in Chapter 10. (The plan's §4.4 calls the rope-swing a "Chapter 9" beat; the swing itself is in Chapter 8, line 421. The plan is one chapter off there.)

## 1.2 Diagnosis

**The cause chain is good. The frequency is not.**

The chain has real meaning: the Agni Kai burns are Ozai's fire, taken while shielding Zuko; the Chapter 5 burn is **Zuko's** fire, taken while gripping his wrists to stop him; the rope damage is taken while doing work no one else can do. Jake's hands are the price of stopping Zuko and carrying the ship. That is a motif worth having.

But because a new wound arrives nearly every chapter, three things break:

1. **Habituation.** Ch8 should be the peak. It cannot be, because Ch6 already said "the palm was a ruin" and Ch9 tears it again.
2. **Timeline.** These are three-year-old scars. Mature burn scars contract, crack in cold, and lose sensation; they do not reopen from ordinary rope work every few days and then heal enough to haul again.
3. **Character.** Jake's hands are his bending instrument, his livelihood, and the proof of his usefulness. A Jake who has survived three years at sea would have a management routine, not a fresh crisis. His flaw is that he **under-reports** cost, not that he neglects treatment.

The repetition also dilutes the intimacy beat. Someone tends Jake's hands in Ch6 (himself), Ch7 (Iroh's look), Ch8 (Zuko sees the blood), Ch9 (Iroh salves), and Ch10 (Zuko rebinds). Five times in five chapters.

One clarification on causation, because it matters for Zuko's guilt: **the Agni Kai burns are Ozai's fire, not Zuko's.** Canon.md is explicit that Jake throws himself into the line of attack and is burned intervening. Zuko's guilt is that Jake paid for him, not that Zuko burned him. Chapter 5 is the one place where Zuko's own fire does the damage, and it lands harder because it is singular.

## 1.3 Proposed hand doctrine

**Baseline (chronic — use freely as texture, never as a new wound):**
- Scar bands tight across the palm; fingers do not fully extend first thing in the morning.
- Cold stiffens them; wet cold is worse. Dry heat cracks them.
- Fingertips partly numb. He cannot feel fine textures through the scar — already used well with Haru's braided cord in Ch8.
- Grip fatigues fast; he swaps hands, uses forearms and elbows, or braces instead of gripping.
- Ache before weather; contracture ache after a growth spurt (relevant if the age changes).
- He wears Iroh's gloves for labour and cold, not only to hide the scars.

**Acute (rationed — one per act, always caused by a choice):**
- Ch5: Zuko's fire.
- Ch6: the consequence of Ch5 plus rope work. The public unveiling stays; the "ruin" hyperbole goes.
- Ch8: the peak. Rope, weight, and the fissure.
- Ch13: arrow, not hands.
- Ch18: explosion.
- Ch20: cold and the final fight.

No new acute hand injury in Ch7, Ch9, or Ch10.

**Management on the page:**
- He wraps **before** heavy work, not after.
- He delegates hauling when he can, and takes it himself when he cannot — the damage is a choice, not carelessness.
- A small kit: salve, clean linen, a needle, spare gloves.
- The basin ritual from Ch5 becomes a nightly habit: water drawn up over the scars, in private, behind a locked door. It is management, it is the locked first-waterbending behaviour made habitual, and it is a secret he performs every night on a Fire Nation ship.

**Failure mode:** he does not skip treatment. He hides the cost. That is consistent with `Core_Persona.md`: worth tied to usefulness, pain minimised, needs not burdened on others. He keeps his hands working because working is how he stays worth keeping, and he says nothing because saying something is the expensive part.

## 1.4 Chapter triage

| Ch | Keep | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Ache under gloves at the harbour | Nothing |
| 4 | Tremor after the Unagi | Trim if it repeats Ch5 |
| 5 | Burn grip, basin | Nothing — this is the best hand beat in the book |
| 6 | Splits, public glove removal, crew silence | Cut "the palm was a ruin"; it is a consequence, not a new ruin |
| 7 | Iroh's look at the gloves | Remove new damage; show wrapping and favouring the hand |
| 8 | Split wrap, blood on the rope, fissure | Keep as peak; use the rope/knife staging already proposed, not another tear |
| 9 | Iroh salve and rewrap | Cut the fresh tear during the fight. Show him fighting without grip instead: forearms, elbows, water, no holds |
| 10 | Picking relapse, Zuko's rebind | Keep the picking; no further reopening. The consequence is that Zuko sees, not that more blood appears |

**Applied 2026-10-17:** Ch6 hyperbole trimmed; Ch7 converted to management; Ch9's fresh tear removed and replaced with grip-free fighting; Ch10 left as written; State_Tracker corrected to match.

**The Chapter 9 fight is the test case.** A Jake whose palms are open should not be trading grips. He should be keeping his hands open, using his forearms, and bending in short linear cuts because he cannot close his fists. That is more interesting than another torn bandage, and it ties directly to the weapon doctrine: the Heron's bill hooks because Jake cannot afford to grip hard.

## 1.5 What this fixes

- Ch8 becomes the peak again, because nothing after it escalates.
- The motif means something: fire he chose to take hold of.
- Three years of adaptation show on the page instead of vanishing between chapters.
- The hurt/comfort beat is rationed, so Zuko rebinding his hand in Ch10 lands as the exception.

# Part 2 — Ages

## 2.1 The current lock is an explicit AU change

`Canon.md` lines 10–12:

> Jake and Zuko are **15** at the time of the Agni Kai and the beginning of exile.
> Jake and Zuko are therefore **18 at the start of Book 1**.

`Writing_Rules.md` line 44:

> **Enforce the AU age progression: Jake and Zuko are 18 years old during Book 1. Do not use ATLA canon ages.**

This is not drift. It is a deliberate divergence, and the prose follows it: Ch1 "eighteen years"; Ch3 "He had been fifteen the last time..." and "He was eighteen now"; Ch8 "as he had at fifteen" (Zuko, at the Agni Kai). Everything is internally consistent at 18/15.

So the question is not which is correct. It is whether to **reverse a locked choice**.

## 2.2 Why the AU may have aged them up

- Adult protagonists for darker material.
- A buffer for the eventual romance (`Canon.md`: "eventual love"; `Relationships.md`: romance after reconciliation in Book 3).
- Jake's authority over a crew of grown men.
- Zuko as a credible threat rather than a child.

Those are real gains, and they are the reason the lock exists.

## 2.3 Three options

| | Agni Kai | Book 1 | Changes | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep 18** | both 15 | both 18 | none | Adult protagonists; peer group older than everyone else; canon Zuko age abandoned |
| **B. Canon Zuko, older Jake** | Zuko 13, Jake 15 | Zuko 16, Jake 18 | Zuko's age only; Ch8 line | Canon Zuko; Jake as older protector; two-year gap; romance 20/18 in Book 3 |
| **C. Both canon** | both 13 | both 16 | 6 base files, 5 prose lines | Exact peer mirror; canon ages; two 13-year-olds exiled; romance 18/18 in Book 3 |

## 2.4 Recommendation: C, with B as the low-cost compromise

**C is better for this story** because the premise is two boys fused by one event. Same age makes the mirror exact: one prince, one servant, both thirteen, both destroyed in the same room, both given the same ship. The class gap already supplies the hierarchy; age should not supply a second one.

It also fixes a register problem. Zuko at 18 can read as stunted — his volatility, shame, and obsession are calibrated to sixteen. At sixteen they are age-appropriate and still painful. And the peer group lines up: Azula 14, Mai and Ty Lee 15, Jet 16, Sokka 15, Katara 14. At 18 Jake and Zuko are the oldest people in their own generation for no story reason.

**B is the pragmatic alternative** if you want to keep Jake's current voice and the protector dynamic at minimal cost. Canon Zuko is restored, Jake stays 18, and only one prose line changes. The cost is that they stop being peers: Jake becomes the older servant who chose a younger prince, which is a different and also valid story.

**A remains defensible** if the adult buffer matters more than canon fidelity. In that case nothing changes, and Ch8's "fifteen" is already correct.

## 2.6 The one cost C does not avoid

At sixteen it still works — three years of exile from thirteen will do that to someone — but the crew's deference may need a line of justification, and a few reflective passages may want to sound a little less settled. That is a re-tune, not a rewrite.
