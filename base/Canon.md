# Canon.md

## Project Scope
- **Project format:** A full retelling of *Avatar: The Last Airbender* across **Books 1–3**, rewritten as a novel-length story centered on **Jake Namiq** and **Zuko**.
- **Current active focus:** Book 1 is the immediate drafting priority, but canon rules in this file apply to the **entire trilogy**.
- **Adaptation model:** This is **not** a scene-by-scene prose novelization of ATLA episodes. It is a **Jake-and-Zuko-centered retelling** that uses canon as structural backbone while shifting narrative focus, emotional emphasis, and scene selection.
- **Canon relationship:** The project remains tied to ATLA’s major plot spine, but it does not need to dramatize every canon event if that event does not materially affect Jake, Zuko, or the consequences surrounding them.

## Exile Timeline Baseline
- Jake and Zuko are **13** at the time of the Agni Kai and the beginning of exile.
- The project follows the original ATLA timeline in which approximately **three years pass between Zuko’s banishment and the opening of Book 1**.
- Jake and Zuko are therefore **16 at the start of Book 1**.
- **Immediate post-Agni-Kai / early exile** and **Book 1 baseline** should be treated as distinct periods in future files.
- Book 1 relationship and character-state material should assume Jake and Zuko have already spent **three years** living in exile together.
- **Exile and hunting are two different clocks (Locked ruling 2026-10-17):** exile lasts **three years**; the active Avatar hunt within that span lasts **two years** (Zhao, Episode 3: "Two years at sea"; Zuko: "I've been hunting the Avatar for two years"). The first year of exile precedes the hunt proper. Prose may say *exiled three years* freely, but never *hunting for three years* — the hunt is two.
- **First waterbending (Locked ruling 2026-10-17):** the Agni Kai is Jake's **first manifestation** — involuntary, public, and witnessed. Year 1 at sea is his **first conscious, deliberate use** — private and instinctive, when he cools and soothes his own burned hands. These are different firsts; no file may conflate them.
- **Story_Bible.md** is the project's authoritative source for detailed chronology within this three-year period. `Canon.md` establishes only the fixed timeline boundaries and continuity constraints.

## Core Premise
- This story shifts the center of ATLA away from Team Avatar as the sole narrative focus and toward **two boys whose lives are fused by the Agni Kai**.
- The project follows how **Jake and Zuko move through exile, war, loyalty, guilt, fracture, reconciliation, and eventual love** while the canon events of ATLA continue around them.
- The story’s purpose is **not** to replace ATLA’s canon structure with an entirely new plot. Its purpose is to **reframe that structure through Jake’s presence and through Jake and Zuko’s evolving relationship**, allowing canon events to be re-experienced from a different emotional center.

## Story Engine
- The central engine of the project is the long-term relationship between **Jake and Zuko**: two boys bound by the Agni Kai and shaped by the same punishment, but not in the same way.
- Their story is driven by:
  - shared exile but unequal burdens
  - loyalty expressed through protection, sacrifice, and silence
  - guilt over what was survived, caused, or failed to prevent
  - the tension between love, dependence, resentment, and obligation
  - the gradual shift from survival-based attachment to a healthier, chosen bond
- ATLA canon events remain important because they repeatedly pressure-test Jake and Zuko’s bond, force new fractures, and create the conditions for change.

## Adaptation Philosophy
### What this retelling is doing
- Re-center the narrative around **Jake and Zuko** while preserving the recognizability of ATLA’s canon arc.
- Use Jake’s presence to reshape:
  - the emotional meaning of canon scenes
  - the private consequences of public events
  - the pacing and texture of Zuko’s exile arc
  - the way Team Avatar encounters, judges, mistrusts, and eventually understands Jake and Zuko
- Treat canon as a **story framework** rather than a scene list to be novelized.

### What this retelling is not doing
- It is **not** a full prose conversion of every episode.
- It is **not** intended to shift ATLA into a Jake-centered replacement plot where canon characters lose their core narrative roles.
- It is **not** meant to turn Jake into a chosen-one figure, secret solution, or hidden key to the war.

## Canon Inclusion Rules
### Scenes that should be fully dramatized
Canon events or original scenes should be shown in full when they:
- directly involve **Jake**
- directly involve **Zuko** in a way that materially affects Jake, or vice versa
- materially alter Jake and Zuko’s relationship
- change Jake’s standing with Team Avatar, Iroh, or other key characters
- establish or pay off emotional, political, or practical consequences that remain relevant later

### Scenes that may be condensed, summarized, or omitted
Canon material does **not** need full dramatization when it:
- does not involve Jake or Zuko directly
- does not materially affect Jake, Zuko, or their relationship
- functions mainly as canon episode business already familiar from ATLA and adds no new Jake/Zuko relevance

### Exception
- A Team Avatar scene may still be included if it materially concerns Jake or Zuko, such as discussing them, reacting to their actions, making plans because of them, or reinterpreting events involving them.

## Canon-Divergence Rule
### Default rule
- The project follows **emotional divergence, structural convergence**.

### In practice, this means:
Jake’s presence may significantly alter:
- emotional context, private conversations, motivations, and misunderstandings
- relationship pacing and the immediate fallout of canon scenes
- some local event logistics and scene order

But the project should still preserve the **major ATLA backbone** unless a later project decision explicitly overrides it.

### Canon divergence is allowed when it:
- grows naturally from Jake’s presence
- deepens the emotional logic of a canon event
- creates additional consequences without breaking the project’s larger canon spine
- meaningfully changes how Jake and Zuko experience the war while still returning to the larger ATLA trajectory

### Canon divergence should be treated cautiously when it would:
- replace major canon character functions with Jake
- erase the original pressure points of ATLA’s plot
- make the story feel like a separate AU that only loosely resembles canon
- simplify canon conflicts by giving Jake outsized problem-solving power

## Jake’s Narrative Role
- Jake is an **active participant**, not a passive witness or emotional observer attached to canon from the outside.
- His role is to **contribute meaningfully** to the story’s events, emotional consequences, and relationship dynamics.
- He can alter the shape of scenes, influence decisions, create fallout, and produce temporary divergences.
- He is allowed to matter.

### But Jake’s role has hard limits
Jake may **not**:
- replace Aang as the structural protagonist of the war
- become the decisive solution to canon conflicts that belong to canon characters
- function as a hidden “fix-it key” who makes canon problems easier than they should be
- absorb or erase the agency of Zuko, Katara, Aang, Iroh, or other canon characters by taking over their emotional or plot functions
- create divergences so large that the story stops feeling anchored to ATLA’s canon backbone

## Narrative Center and POV Rules
- **Narrative mode:** third-person limited.
- **Primary POV anchor:** Jake.
- **Recurring secondary POV anchor:** Zuko.
- Other POV characters may be used **sparingly** if a scene materially benefits from them, but the project should not drift into a wide ensemble POV structure by default.
- Even when other POVs appear, the story’s emotional center should remain aligned with **Jake first, Zuko second, and the Jake/Zuko relationship as the project’s main axis**.

## Jake and Zuko Relationship Arc
### Relationship status by book
#### Book 1
- No romance.
- The priority is building the **foundational bond**, not romantic payoff.
- Book 1 should establish:
  - the **long aftermath** of the Agni Kai as the shared origin trauma that shaped the three years of exile before Book 1
  - the emotional shape of exile
  - how Jake and Zuko function together under pressure
  - where loyalty, dependence, resentment, guilt, and protectiveness already exist by the start of Book 1
  - what is unsaid between them

#### Book 2
- Their bond deepens, but it is **not yet fully romantic**.
- Emotional intimacy, reliance, and vulnerability may increase.
- The story should move toward an eventual **falling out / rupture**, rather than toward early resolution.

#### Book 3
- Reconciliation comes before romance.
- Their friendship and trust must be repaired into something healthier before romance is allowed to become explicit.
- Romance is **endgame**, but it should feel earned through repair, accountability, and changed behavior rather than through unresolved dependency alone.

## Romance Rule
- Jake/Zuko is a **romantic endgame pairing**.
- However, the project should not frame their dynamic as an active romance in Book 1.
- Romance should not override the foundational work of trust, conflict, co-dependence vs genuine care, guilt, exile survival, and reconciliation after betrayal.

## Thematic Priorities
### Identity and belonging
- Jake’s split between **Water Tribe origin** and **Fire Nation upbringing**
- what “home” means when legal identity, cultural identity, and emotional loyalty do not point to the same place
- how much of the self can be built from survival rather than inheritance

### Exile as lived experience
- Exile should not function only as a plot condition.
- It should be treated as a routine, embodied reality: hunger, instability, shame, travel, dependence, improvisation, and the strain of living without a secure place to belong.

### The emotional cost of loyalty
- Protection, obedience, sacrifice, silence, and self-erasure should all be treated as forms of loyalty with different costs.
- The story should examine when loyalty becomes care, when it becomes fear, and when it becomes a trap.

### Public events vs private aftermath
- ATLA’s large canon events matter, but this project is especially interested in what happens **around** them: the private conversations after a public humiliation, the emotional fallout after a battle, the quiet consequences of betrayal, rescue, exile, and forgiveness.

### Healing after betrayal
- The story should not treat reconciliation as automatic. Betrayal, abandonment, divided loyalties, and damaged trust should have real duration and consequence before repair.

### Warmth alongside pain
- The project should preserve ATLA’s ability to hold grief, humor, awkwardness, affection, and absurdity in the same story. Emotional heaviness should not flatten the world into uninterrupted angst.

## End-State Targets
### Jake’s internal arc
- Jake has stopped measuring his worth solely through usefulness.
- He does not need to earn belonging only by protecting, fixing, or sacrificing himself.
- His identity is no longer defined only by what he can do for other people.

### Jake and Zuko
- Jake and Zuko choose each other from a healthier place than the bond they began with.
- Their relationship is no longer held together only by guilt, habit, obligation, or shared damage.
- Their romance, if present on-page by that point, should feel like an extension of earned trust and mutual choice rather than unresolved dependency.

### Jake and the wider cast
- Jake is accepted without needing to replace anyone in Team Avatar.
- His place in the group should not require him to become a substitute sibling, replacement son, or superior moral center.
- Haru and Emi remain important to Jake’s larger life and history, but are not currently the project’s primary end-state focus.

## Jake’s Political / Identity Baseline
### Core identity facts
- Jake is a waterbender born in an **unspecified Southern Water Tribe community**. It is not Wolf Cove; the community's exact identity and fate remain unresolved.
- He is later raised in the **Fire Nation** by **Haru and Emi**, who take him in as their son.
- Jake does **not** grow up with a secure, formal noble or state-protected identity. His place in the Fire Nation is practical and lived, not legally stable.

### What Jake knows before the Agni Kai
- Jake knows he is **adopted**.
- He does **not** know he is from a Southern Water Tribe community.
- He does **not** know he is a waterbender.
- Before the Agni Kai, he understands himself primarily as a Fire Nation orphan who was taken in by Haru and Emi. His Fire Nation identity is sincere, lived, and emotionally real, even if it later proves incomplete.

### Haru and Emi’s knowledge baseline
- Haru and Emi know Jake is **not** their biological child and know he is **not** Fire Nation by birth.
- They know enough to understand that Jake’s origins are politically dangerous if exposed.
- They deliberately raise him without telling him the full truth of his origins while he is still a child.
- Their secrecy is protective rather than exploitative, but it still becomes a source of later fracture and grief.

## Jake’s Palace Status Before Exile
### Social position
- Jake is raised in palace orbit as the son of **Haru and Emi**, who work as servants attached to the royal household.
- Jake also works as a servant boy as he grows older.
- He is **not** a formal ward of the state, noble dependent, or publicly recognized child of court importance.
- His place in palace life is tolerated and normalized through routine rather than secured through formal legitimacy.

### Legal / political status
- Jake’s status inside the Fire Nation is effectively **informal and unstable**.
- Haru and Emi still function as Jake’s parents in every practical and emotional sense, but his presence is vulnerable to scrutiny if the wrong people start asking where he came from and whether he legally belongs there.
- Before the Agni Kai, this instability is mostly invisible to Jake because his life has been normalized inside the household and palace routine.
- After the Agni Kai, that same instability becomes one of the reasons he can be reframed as an illegitimate, dangerous presence and removed with little protection.

## Jake and Zuko Before the Agni Kai
### First meeting / early contact
- Jake first begins crossing paths with Zuko at approximately **age 6–7**, after he has already spent several years in Haru and Emi’s care.
- Their earliest encounters happen through palace routine rather than dramatic fate: errands, deliveries, stray moments in hallways, gardens, courtyards, training-adjacent spaces, or other servant-prince overlap points.
- The relationship does **not** begin as instant best friendship. It grows gradually through repeated proximity.

### Friendship timeline
- Their early relationship is uneven and shaped by class imbalance, curiosity, irritation, and repeated encounters rather than instant intimacy.
- Their relationship shifts into genuine friendship at approximately **age ten or eleven**, once Jake becomes a consistent presence and Zuko starts seeking him out rather than merely tolerating him.
- By the Agni Kai at **13**, they already have several years of shared routine, familiarity, and meaningful emotional reliance behind them, even if some of the deeper unhealthy dependence between them develops during exile.

## Pre-Agni Kai Emotional Baseline Between Jake and Zuko
- Jake and Zuko are **not** immediate childhood soulmates and should not be written as inseparable from the start.
- By the Agni Kai, they are emotionally important to each other, but the bond has formed gradually across years of palace life.
- Jake is one of the few people around whom Zuko does not have to perform competence or princely composure at all times.
- Zuko is one of the few people Jake feels consistently seen by before exile.
- Their pre-Agni-Kai bond should already matter enough that Jake’s intervention feels emotionally inevitable, but not so fully fused that the exile years have no room left to deepen or distort the relationship.

## Jake’s Latent Waterbending Before the Agni Kai
### Knowledge baseline
- Jake does **not** knowingly practice waterbending before the Agni Kai.
- He does **not** understand himself to be a waterbender.
- Haru and Emi may have noticed scattered warning signs over the years, but Jake himself does not have a stable category for them.

### Warning-sign logic
- Any pre-Agni-Kai hints of waterbending should remain **subtle, intermittent, and easy for Jake to rationalize away**.
- They should not read like Jake secretly training or repeatedly performing obvious supernatural acts without drawing attention.
- The purpose of these signs is continuity texture, not retroactive proof that Jake consciously knew what he was.

### Tone of those incidents
- They should feel like: strange accidents, emotionally charged moments, impossible near-misses, or things Haru or Emi privately worry about more than Jake does.
- Emi, in particular, may treat some of these moments with private unease because she already suspects Jake’s origins are not what he believes.

## The Agni Kai Turning Point
### Event function
- The Agni Kai is the foundational rupture point of the project.
- It destroys Zuko’s standing, exposes Jake’s hidden identity, and binds both boys to the same punishment event in different ways.
- It is the moment where private attachment becomes public catastrophe.

### What Jake does
- During Ozai’s punishment of Zuko, Jake intervenes physically to protect him.
- Jake throws himself into the line of attack in an attempt to shield Zuko from worse injury.
- In the process, Jake instinctively waterbends for the first time or for the first clearly visible time.
- Jake’s hands are burned in the process of intervening, leaving him with burn scars tied directly to the Agni Kai.

### What the intervention means
Jake’s act is simultaneously:
- a public disruption of the Fire Lord’s punishment
- a direct act of defiance against Ozai
- the exposure of a hidden waterbender inside palace orbit
- proof of Jake’s loyalty to Zuko in the worst possible setting

## Agni Kai Witness Scope
- Jake’s waterbending is **not** treated as a perfectly private secret after the Agni Kai, but neither is it necessarily witnessed in clean detail by every person in court.
- The working canon assumption is a **limited but decisive witness circle**:
  - **Ozai** knows what happened
  - **Iroh** knows what happened
  - **Zuko** knows what happened
  - at least some additional court witnesses or guards understand enough that the event cannot simply be dismissed or erased
- **Azula is close enough to know that something deeply irregular happened during the Agni Kai, but she does not necessarily get a perfect, instant read on Jake’s identity in the moment.**
- Azula later pieces together the truth through the fallout, palace knowledge, or post-Agni-Kai investigation rather than receiving it as a fully clean visual revelation inside the scene itself.
- This means Jake’s reveal is politically consequential and dangerous, but not staged as a perfectly public courtroom announcement to every future enemy at once.

## Why Jake Is Not Executed
Jake is **not** spared because his crime is minor, and he is **not** spared because Ozai is merciful. He survives because **Iroh successfully reframes Jake’s punishment in a way that better serves Ozai’s interests than execution would**.

### Iroh’s core reframing
Iroh prevents Jake from being treated as an independent political actor or organized threat. Instead, he frames Jake as:
- a servant boy in palace orbit
- acting rashly out of personal attachment to Zuko
- guilty of defiance, but not part of a larger conspiracy
- inseparable from the larger disgrace already unfolding around Zuko

This matters because if Jake is treated as a standalone case, execution is the obvious response. Iroh’s goal is to stop Jake from becoming a separate political spectacle and instead fold his offense into **Zuko’s punishment**.

### Why execution is politically inconvenient
Iroh’s argument to Ozai rests on the idea that a formal execution would give Jake and the incident **too much attention**. Executing Jake would force the court to dwell on a palace embarrassment:
- a hidden waterbender servant lived in palace orbit
- palace staff concealed or failed to expose him
- the Agni Kai did not remain a perfectly controlled act of punishment
- someone was loyal enough to Zuko to physically interfere with the Fire Lord’s judgment

From Ozai’s perspective, this is not a reason to forgive Jake. It is a reason to avoid turning Jake into a second focal point of the Agni Kai.

### Why exile serves Ozai better
By exiling Jake with Zuko, Ozai can:
- remove Jake from the palace immediately
- fold Jake’s offense into Zuko’s disgrace instead of elevating it into a separate court event
- erase Jake from palace life without granting him the attention of a public execution
- make Jake part of Zuko’s punishment rather than a separate enemy of the state
- force both boys to continue living inside the consequences of the Agni Kai instead of ending Jake’s suffering quickly

Under this logic, exile is not mercy. It is disposal, humiliation, and prolonged punishment.

### Iroh’s practical maneuver
Iroh does not just argue abstractly. He also makes exile **administratively convenient**.
- Iroh publicly intervenes to prevent immediate execution or a separate court condemnation.
- Afterward, he privately maneuvers to secure exile as the formal outcome.
- Iroh assumes responsibility for Jake as part of Zuko’s exile party, making Jake’s removal from court immediate and politically manageable.

## Jake’s Punishment After the Agni Kai
### Official logic of punishment
Jake is not punished as a noble traitor. He is punished as:
- an unlawful or illegitimate presence in palace orbit
- a servant boy who interfered in an Agni Kai
- someone who directly defied the Fire Lord
- a concealed waterbender whose presence inside the palace becomes intolerable once exposed

### Practical outcome
- Jake is removed from Haru and Emi’s protection.
- His place in palace life is retroactively reframed as illegitimate and dangerous.
- Zuko’s banishment is the **publicly declared** punishment.
- Jake’s punishment is **not** given equal public ceremony or a matching court spectacle.
- Jake’s exile is instead an **immediate private or semi-private secondary order** issued in the aftermath of the Agni Kai and communicated through palace authority.
- Exile functions as both punishment and erasure: Jake loses home, status, security, and belonging in the same moment that Zuko loses his.

## Jake’s Immediate Post-Agni Kai Knowledge
- Jake does **not** leave the Agni Kai with a perfectly clear, fully verbalized understanding of himself as a waterbender.
- He remembers the event in **fragments at first**.
- He knows he intervened, knows he was burned, and knows something impossible happened, but he does **not** instantly process waterbending as a stable identity category.
- The realization of what he is unfolds across the **early exile period**, rather than arriving as one neat revelation in the Agni Kai chamber itself.

## Zuko’s Immediate Post-Agni Kai Knowledge
### Immediate aftermath
Zuko knows:
- Jake threw himself in front of him
- Jake was burned because of that intervention
- something impossible happened in the moment Jake stepped in
- the palace’s reaction to Jake becomes far more severe than it would be if Jake had only committed an ordinary act of interference

### Early exile understanding
- Zuko does **not** leave the Agni Kai with a clean official explanation of Jake’s identity or origins.
- Instead, he gradually realizes that the adults, especially Iroh, know more than they are saying, and that Jake’s punishment is tied to more than simply interrupting Ozai.
- Zuko understands very early that Jake’s intervention has exposed something dangerous, but he does **not** immediately know the full Southern Water Tribe / hidden waterbender / palace-secret dimension of it.

## Jake’s Early-Exile Communication Pattern
- Jake does **not** enter exile with a coherent explanation of what happened to give Zuko.
- He cannot fully explain the Agni Kai because he does not understand it himself.
- In the early exile period, Jake tends to give **fragments rather than a full confession**.
- He minimizes his own fear, pain, and confusion rather than volunteering them.
- He avoids directly discussing the possibility that he is not Fire Nation by birth until the evidence becomes harder to ignore.
- The result is a communication gap between Jake and Zuko in early exile, driven less by deliberate deception than by shame, confusion, fear, and Jake’s habit of swallowing distress instead of naming it.

## Jake’s Keepsake Baseline (Locked)
- Jake leaves the Fire Nation with **one small personal keepsake**: a simple handmade braided cord charm created by Haru.
- The object is emotionally intimate and materially ordinary rather than mythic, political, or destiny-coded.
- It represents Haru's stability and Emi's care, anchoring Jake to his pre-exile life.
- It has no special abilities, hidden meaning, or connection to bending. It exists solely as a deeply personal reminder of the home and family Jake lost.

## Iroh’s Relationship to Jake
- Iroh has a meaningful prior relationship with Jake before the Agni Kai, built through palace proximity, Haru and Emi, Zuko’s orbit, or a combination of the three.
- Iroh’s attachment to Jake is strong enough that he actively intervenes to save Jake’s life after the Agni Kai.
- Iroh’s role in Jake’s survival should be treated as **deliberate, politically costly, and emotionally significant**, not incidental.

## Canon Anchors Currently Presumed Intact
Unless later project notes explicitly override them, the following broad ATLA anchors are presumed to remain in place:
- the story still spans **Books 1–3** of ATLA
- the broad structure of canon events remains recognizable
- Zuko’s banishment and exile still occur
- Team Avatar remains central to the war effort and to the canon backbone of the series
- Jake’s presence changes emotional and situational dynamics, but does not replace the major ATLA arc structure by default

## Later-Divergence Policy
- Stronger divergence in Books 2–3 is allowed **only** when it directly grows from Jake’s established presence, materially affects Jake/Zuko’s relationship or trust arc, and still preserves the broader ATLA end-state spine unless explicitly re-approved.
- Books 2–3 divergence points should be revisited **after Book 1 continuity and emotional arc files are stabilized**.
- Approval of later divergence should favor the smallest departure from canon necessary to preserve emotional causality.
- The following areas are recognized as likely future divergence pressure points, but are **not yet fully locked**:
  - **The Blue Spirit / early Book 1 rescue dynamics**
  - **Book 2 fracture / betrayal material**
  - **How Team Avatar gradually recontextualizes Zuko through Jake**
  - **Post-Agni Kai conversations or flashback scenes that canon never showed**

## Hard Guardrails
The project should **not** drift into the following:
- **Jake as chosen one / hidden savior:** Jake should not become a secret key to the war, destiny figure, or replacement protagonist for ATLA’s core canon role.
- **Biological-family mystery as the main plot:** Jake’s birth family or origin may matter, but the project should not reframe itself around uncovering his bloodline as its central engine.
- **Romance too early:** Jake/Zuko romance should not be foregrounded in Book 1. Early emotional intensity should not be mistaken for a fully romantic dynamic before the relationship has earned that shift.
- **Pure angst at the expense of ATLA’s range:** The project should not flatten the tone into nonstop suffering, melodrama, or emotional misery. Humor, awkwardness, domesticity, tenderness, and ordinary life should still have room to exist.
- **Canon displacement:** Jake should not absorb the narrative jobs of multiple canon characters simply because he is the OC lead. Canon characters should remain fully realized agents.
